# SESC-IMS-06 · Planning

**Clause 6 of ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018 — how the Employer decides what
could go wrong and what could go better, what it must comply with, what it will achieve, and how
it plans a change before making it.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 26 October 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This document is issued for review by the Chief Operating Officer, for consultation with the
> Employer's non-managerial workers on the objectives under SESC-IMS-05 Part 5, and for approval
> by the Managing Director. **The objectives at Part 6 are proposed, not set.** Setting them is the
> Managing Director's (SESC-POL-16 ¶6; SESC-POL-05 ¶8(d); SESC-POL-08 ¶6), and until he does,
> **environmental and occupational health and safety objectives have not been set** (CLAUDE.md

## Part 1 — Purpose, and how this chapter is built

1. This document is the clause 6 chapter of the spine described at SESC-IMS-00 ¶20. It does not
   hold the risks, the opportunities, the aspects or the obligations: those are data, held in the
   registers so that they can be scored, owned, dated and reported, and this document says how
   each register is used in planning and points at it. What this document does hold is the one
   thing no register holds — the Employer's objectives, and the plan to achieve them — and the
   rule for planning a change.

2. **Scope of application.** This document applies to all Staff of the Employer, irrespective of
   seniority, tenure and working hours, including all employees & subcontractors, directors and
   officers, consultants and contractors, casual or agency staff, apprentices, trainees, homeworkers
   and fixed-term staff and any volunteers.

3. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function reserved to
   the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will be performed by
   the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

4. **Words used.** "A risk" is the effect of uncertainty on what the Employer is trying to achieve;
   "an opportunity" is a circumstance that could improve its performance; the two are kept in
   separate registers and separate columns and are never merged (CLAUDE.md §6; SESC-REG-08). "A
   hazard" is a source with the potential to cause injury or ill health; "an aspect" is an element
   of the Employer's activities that interacts with the environment. "An objective" is a result to
   be achieved, specific, measurable, owned, resourced and dated; "an objective expressed as
   'ongoing', with no number and no date, is not an objective and will not be accepted into the
   plan" (SESC-POL-08 ¶62). "Baseline" is the measured starting position with the date it was
   measured; where nothing has been measured the baseline is recorded as not measured, never
   estimated.

## Part 2 — Actions to address risks and opportunities (clause 6.1.1; ISO 9001 clause 6.1.2)

5. **What is considered.** Planning starts from the issues at SESC-IMS-04 Part 4 and SESC-REG-06,
   including the climate change determination at CLI-00; the interested parties and their
   requirements at SESC-IMS-04 Part 5 and SESC-REG-05; and the scope at SESC-IMS-04 Part 3. From
   those the Employer determines what it needs to address so that the system can achieve its
   intended results, prevent or reduce undesired effects, and improve.

6. **Where risks live.** Business risks — commercial, contractual, insurance, competence, supply,
   compliance, reputational and climate-related — are scored on likelihood and impact against a
   stated tolerance in **SESC-REG-01 Business Risk Register**, owned by the Managing Director,
   with every risk outside tolerance carrying an owner and a target date, reviewed quarterly and
   at the management review (SESC-POL-16 ¶65). Risks revealed by the system — repeated
   nonconformities, supplier failures, competence gaps, terms beyond what the Employer can carry —
   are fed into SESC-REG-01 and not held separately (SESC-POL-16 ¶66). Environmental risks to the
   business are SESC-POL-08 E4's and sit in SESC-REG-01 and SESC-REG-06, distinct from the aspects
   register (SESC-POL-08 ¶24).

7. **Where opportunities live.** **SESC-REG-08 Opportunity Register**, draft v0.1, scored with a
   pursuit plan, owner and status, separate from risks (D24). It holds the business opportunities
   SESC-POL-08 ¶27 names — fuel reduction through telematics and route planning, waste reduction
   through take-back and ordering, winning work on environmental credentials, growing the
   renewables and energy-efficiency side — and the quality and system opportunities arising from
   SESC-PRO-02 audits and SESC-PRO-04 investigations.

8. **OH&S opportunities (ISO 45001 clause 6.1.2.3).** The opportunities to improve health and safety
   performance — adapting the work, the organisation and the environment to the worker;
   eliminating a hazard rather than controlling it; a change of method, product or equipment that
   removes an exposure; and the opportunities to improve the system itself — are assessed as they
   arise from the risk assessment review, the consultation mechanism at SESC-IMS-05 Part 5, incident
   investigation and audit, and are entered in SESC-REG-08 tagged OH&S, with the hierarchy of
   control applied to each (SESC-POL-05 A1 ¶20; A13 ¶77; A18 ¶104). **No OH&S opportunity is yet
   entered in SESC-REG-08** (Part 8, item (d)): the register's ten rows at v0.1 were drawn from
   SESC-REG-06, and the OH&S tag is proposed in this document for its v0.2.

9. **Planning the actions (ISO 9001 clause 6.1.2; clause 6.1.4).** For each risk outside tolerance,
   each pursued opportunity, each significant aspect and each compliance obligation, the action is
   recorded in the register that raised it, with its owner and date; it is integrated into the
   process it affects — the quotation and contract review, the RAMS, the readiness review, the
   procurement criteria, the inspection plan — rather than run beside it; and its effectiveness is
   evaluated by the measure named against it and reported at the management review (SESC-PRO-03
   input 4). Where an action is a corrective action it is held on SESC-FRM-05 under SESC-PRO-04;
   where it is a change to a document it goes through SESC-PRO-01; where it needs money it is the
   Managing Director's decision.

## Part 6 — Objectives and planning to achieve them (clause 6.2)

### 6.1 How objectives are set, and what each must carry

13. The Managing Director sets the objectives at the management review (SESC-PRO-03 ¶14(d)), within
    the framework of the integrated policy at SESC-IMS-05 Part 3, taking account of the significant
    aspects, the risks and opportunities, the compliance obligations, the compliance evaluation,
    audit findings, incident history, consultation outcomes and customer expectations (SESC-POL-08
    ¶61). Each objective is **specific, measurable** (or capable of performance evaluation where
    measurement is not practicable), **monitored, communicated, updated as appropriate**, and
    carries: the baseline and the date it was measured; the target and the date; what will be done;
    the resources; the owner (a role); how progress is measured and how the result is evaluated;
    and the review dates (SESC-POL-08 ¶63; SESC-POL-16 ¶67; SESC-POL-05 ¶46).

14. **Consultation.** The objectives are among the matters ISO 45001 clause 5.4 names for
    consultation with non-managerial workers. The health and safety objectives below are put to
    the quarterly consultation meeting (SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(c)) before the Managing Director sets them,
    and what the workforce says is recorded on SESC-FRM-08 and reported to him.

15. **Progress** is reviewed quarterly by the owner and reported to the Managing Director at the
    interim review (SESC-PRO-03 ¶6(b)), and formally at the annual management review, where each
    objective is closed, carried forward or revised and the reason recorded (SESC-POL-08 ¶64). The
    measures are taken from the records the system generates — SESC-FRM-01 to SESC-FRM-08,
    SESC-REG-03, the Waste Register, the environmental data set, the audit reports — and normalised
    against hours worked from the payroll and timesheets where the measure is a rate (SESC-POL-05
    ¶47; SESC-IMS-04 ¶12).

### 6.2 What exists today

16. **Quality.** SESC-POL-16 ¶67 records that the Managing Director sets quality objectives annually
    and names eight measures the Employer monitors: nonconformances raised and closed within target;
    complaints and time to resolve; rework cost as a proportion of turnover; snagging items at
    handover; on-time completion; the proportion of projects with a completed inspection and test
    plan; the proportion with a complete handover pack on the day; and calibration currency.
    SESC-POL-16 ¶74(e) records that objectives under Q23 are set for the year commencing
    1 September 2026 and that no earlier dated set exists. **No document held in the three sources
    states a numeric target or a baseline for any of the eight measures, and no measure has been
    taken** (Part 8, item (b)). The measures are therefore the framework, and the targets at ¶18
    are proposed to give them numbers.

17. **Environment and health and safety.** SESC-POL-05 ¶46 names three standing objectives — zero
    RIDDOR-reportable injuries, 100% current competence across the Training Matrix, and 100%
    completion of planned site inspections — and SESC-POL-08 ¶65 names five standing areas — fuel
    and greenhouse gas reduction aligned to SESC-CRP-01, waste reduction and diversion from
    landfill, elimination of environmental incidents, completion of the audit programme and closure
    of findings, and supply chain due diligence. **Neither document sets a dated, numbered objective
    for the current year, and none has been set at a management review** (SESC-IMS-00 Part 10 (j);
    SESC-IMS-05 Part 6(h)). The proposals at ¶18 give each standing objective and area a baseline
    position, a number and a date.

## Part 7 — Planning of changes (ISO 9001 clause 6.3)

21. A change to the system is planned before it is made. The plan — which may be a paragraph in a
    pull request description, a Master Register entry or a minute — states the purpose of the
    change and its potential consequences; what it does to the integrity of the system (which
    documents, registers, forms and processes it touches); the resources it needs; and who becomes
    responsible for what. Changes to documents go through SESC-PRO-01 ¶15 to ¶18; changes to the
    business — a new trade, a new site type, a new scheme, a change of premises or of standards
    editions — are review triggers for SESC-IMS-04 and SESC-IMS-00 (SESC-IMS-00 ¶34) and are planned
    at the management review (SESC-PRO-03 ¶14(h)); a change arising from a nonconformity is planned
    on SESC-FRM-05 (SESC-PRO-04 ¶16). Where a change bears on health and safety, the ISO 45001
    management-of-change arrangement at clause 8.1.3 applies through the RAMS revision and
    re-briefing at SESC-POL-05 A2 ¶23, and this document does not claim that clause.

22. **The change this document plans.** Decision D27 of 26 October 2026 — drafting the EMS documents
    to the ISO 14001 clause structure held rather than waiting for the purchase of the 2026 edition
    — is itself a planned change: its purpose is to put a draft of every Stage 1 document in front
    of the retained adviser and the certification body without a month's delay; its consequence is
    that every ISO 14001 clause reference in SESC-REG-10, SESC-IMS-06 and SESC-PRO-01 to 04 carries
    a review trigger for the purchase and must be re-read then; its resource is the Chief Operating
    Officer's time; and the certification body's written position on the edition (SESC-IMS-00
    Part 10 (w)) remains the decision that settles it.
