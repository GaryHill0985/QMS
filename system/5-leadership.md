# SESC-IMS-05 · Leadership

**Clause 5 of ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018 — leadership and commitment,
the integrated policy statement, and the consultation and participation of workers.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 7 October 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This document is issued for review by the Chief Operating Officer, for consultation with the
> Employer's non-managerial workers under Part 5, and for approval by the Managing Director. Until
> the Approval block at Part 7 is completed it evidences nothing, **the three policy statements it
> is written to supersede remain in force unchanged, and the consultation mechanism it describes is
> designed, not operating.**
>
> **The Employer holds no ISO certification of any kind and does not claim to.** No certification
> body has ever assessed the Employer. Every statement in this document is written to be true on
> the day it is read, or is marked at Part 6 as a record that does not yet exist, with the date by
> which it will.

---

## Part 1 — Purpose, and what this document does

1. This document states how the Managing Director leads the Integrated Management System and
   is accountable for it, carries the Employer's single policy for quality, environment and health
   and safety, and sets out the mechanism by which the Employer's non-managerial workers are
   consulted on the system and take part in it. It satisfies clause 5 of all three standards to
   which the Employer is building, except for clause 5.3, which is satisfied by SESC-IMS-04 ¶11 and
   is not restated here (Part 4).

2. It is written once, for all three standards, for the reason given at SESC-IMS-04 ¶2: the
   Employer operates one business, from one site, with one workforce, and three policy statements
   signed on different days for one business is three answers to the same question. Where a
   requirement applies to only one standard, the paragraph says so and Annex A tags it.

3. **How this document relates to the signed policies.** The Employer's quality, environmental
   and health and safety policy statements are, at the date of this draft, Part 1 of
   SESC-POL-16 v1.2, Part 1 of SESC-POL-08 v1.1 and Part 1 of SESC-POL-05 v1.1, each signed by
   the Managing Director. The statement at Part 3 is written to replace all three on the Managing
   Director's signature of this document. **Until he signs, the three signed statements stand and
   this document changes nothing.** The organisation and arrangements in Parts 2 and 3 of each of
   those documents are not replaced by this document and remain the operative statements until
   the SESC-IMS-06 to SESC-IMS-10 spine documents are issued (SESC-IMS-04 ¶54).

4. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function
   reserved to the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will
   be performed by the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

5. **Scope of application.** This document applies to all Staff of the Employer, irrespective
   of seniority, tenure and working hours, including all employees & subcontractors, directors
   and officers, consultants and contractors, casual or agency staff, apprentices, trainees,
   homeworkers and fixed-term staff and any volunteers.

6. **Words used.** "The Employer" is SESC Solutions Ltd. "The system" is the Integrated
   Management System described in SESC-IMS-00. "Non-managerial workers" means every person
   working under the Employer's control who does not manage other people's work: the directly
   employed operatives, including the four apprentices, and the operatives of subcontractors
   while they work on the Employer's jobs. The Managing Director, the Chief Operating Officer,
   the Contracts Manager and the Site Supervisors are managerial for the purposes of Part 5,
   and a Site Supervisor is also a worker who is consulted in their own right.

## Part 2 — Leadership and commitment (clause 5.1)

### 2.1 How the Managing Director demonstrates leadership

7. The Managing Director takes accountability for the effectiveness of the system, for the
   prevention of work-related injury and ill health, for the protection of the environment, and
   for the conformity of the work the Employer delivers. He cannot delegate that accountability.
   What he does, and where the record of it is, is set out below. Where no record yet exists,
   Part 6 says so with a date.

8. **He sets the policy and the objectives.** The policy is at Part 3, and it is his signature
   at Part 7 that makes it the Employer's. The objectives are set at the management review, which
   he chairs. Quality objectives are set for the year commencing 1 September 2026 under
   SESC-POL-16 Q23; **environmental and health and safety objectives have not yet been set**
   (Part 6, item (h)).

9. **He provides the resources.** People, time, premises, plant, vehicles, equipment, training
   and the retained external health and safety adviser. Under SESC-POL-16 Q20 ¶63(d) an improvement
   action with no time allocated to it is either resourced or closed with the reason recorded.
   Under Part 5 of this document, consultation takes place in paid time.

10. **He integrates the system into the way the business runs**, rather than beside it: the
    quotation states what is and is not offered; no job starts without a risk assessment and
    method statement; competence is checked before work is allocated; the test and commissioning
    records are the release of the work. Those are business processes first and system
    requirements second, and they are described in SESC-POL-16, SESC-POL-05 and SESC-POL-08.

11. **He approves and signs every controlled document.** No controlled document is issued
    without his signature, and a change to one is authored and reviewed by the Chief Operating
    Officer and approved by him (SESC-IMS-04 ¶53). The twenty-one signed documents listed in
    SESC-IMS-00 Annex B carry his signature, each verified on 17 August 2026.

12. **He chairs the management review**, at least annually, with the inputs and outputs described
    at SESC-IMS-00 ¶58. **No management review has been minuted since 10 January 2023.** This is
    the one record that shows leadership operating rather than being written down, and it does
    not yet exist (Part 6, item (g)).

13. **He directs and supports people.** Every operative and every Site Supervisor has the
    authority, and the duty, to stop work that cannot be carried out safely, and no person will
    suffer any detriment for exercising it or for raising a concern in good faith
    (SESC-IMS-04 ¶13). A worker who raises something is answered, under Part 5.

14. **He leads the culture.** The Employer's stated preference is to say what is not true about
    its own records rather than conceal it (SESC-IMS-04 ¶44), and that preference is his: it is
    why every management-system document carries a dated inventory of the records it does not yet
    have. Falsifying an inspection, test, training, attendance or accident record is gross
    misconduct under SESC-POL-16 ¶76 and SESC-POL-05 ¶35(g). The ethics and culture commitment at
    Part 3 carries this into the policy.

15. **He ensures that workers are consulted and take part.** That is a requirement of ISO 45001
    clause 5.1 as well as of clause 5.4, and the mechanism is at Part 5. The Managing Director
    attends the quarterly consultation meeting at ¶38(c) and receives the monthly report of open
    items at ¶38(d). **The mechanism has not yet operated, and he has not yet decided whether to
    offer the workforce an elected representative** (Part 6, items (b) and (e)).

16. **He is personally exposed if the system is not real.** Section 37 of the Health and Safety
    at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 attach to
    him as sole director (SESC-REG-05, the Managing Director as an interested party). A functioning
    ISO 45001 system is the mechanism that protects him, and that is recorded here because it is
    the plainest reason the system has to work rather than merely exist.

### 2.2 Customer focus (ISO 9001 clause 5.1.2)

17. The Managing Director ensures that what the customer requires, and what the law and the
    schemes require of the work, is determined before the Employer commits, and is met. The
    control is the contract review at SESC-POL-16 Q2 ¶14, carried out by the Contracts Manager
    and, where any of the matters at ¶14(a) to (g) arise, by the Managing Director, who takes the
    decision on terms, insurance limits and obligations beyond what the Employer holds. Risks and
    opportunities that affect the conformity of the work are held in SESC-REG-01 and SESC-REG-06.

18. Complaints are handled under SESC-POL-16 Q15 and are an input to the management review.
    **Customer satisfaction is not yet monitored as a distinct input** (SESC-IMS-00 Part 10(t);
    Part 6, item (j)). Until it is, the Employer's evidence of customer focus is the contract
    review and complaints records held per job, and this document does not claim more.

## Part 3 — The integrated policy statement (clause 5.2)

### 3.1 What the statement must do, and what was read before it was written

19. Each of the three standards requires a policy that is appropriate to the Employer's purpose
    and context, gives a framework for setting objectives, commits the Employer to meeting its
    requirements and obligations and to continual improvement, is held as documented
    information, is communicated and understood within the Employer, and is available to
    interested parties. ISO 14001 adds the protection of the environment, including the
    prevention of pollution, and the fulfilment of compliance obligations. ISO 45001 adds safe and
    healthy working conditions for the prevention of work-related injury and ill health, the
    elimination of hazards and reduction of risks, and the consultation and participation of
    workers and, where they exist, workers' representatives. **The ISO 14001 requirements are
    read from the clause map carried over from the withdrawn 2015 edition, because nobody on this
    project has read ISO 14001:2026** (Part 6, item (l)). The statement is written to be ready for
    ISO 9001:2026, which adds an expectation of an ethics and quality-culture commitment, and it
    carries the climate change determination recorded at SESC-REG-06 CLI-00.

20. **The three signed statements were read in full before this one was written** — every page
    of SESC-POL-16 v1.2 (21 pages, signed 16 August 2026), SESC-POL-05 v1.1 (32 pages, signed
    16 August 2026) and SESC-POL-08 v1.1 (23 pages, signed 14 August 2026), on 7 October 2026.
    Every commitment in the three Part 1 statements is carried into the statement below, so that
    the Employer's policy becomes shorter but not weaker. Where it is carried is shown at ¶29.

21. **What the statement does not do.** It asserts no record. It does not say that the Employer
    monitors, audits, measures or reviews anything in the present tense; it commits the Employer
    to doing so, and the records of it being done are inventoried, with dates, at Part 6 and at
    SESC-IMS-00 Part 10. It names no headcount, turnover or insurance limit. It claims no
    certification.

### 3.2 The statement

22. **Integrated policy for quality, environment and health and safety.** SESC Solutions Ltd
    (the Employer, we, our or us) carries out roofing, building fabric, mechanical, electrical
    and renewable energy works, and mould and damp remediation, largely in existing and occupied
    buildings and on customer and Government establishments. Three things decide whether we
    deserve the work: that it is right and lasts; that nobody is hurt or made ill doing it or
    living with it; and that it leaves the environment no worse than we found it. This policy
    covers all three in one statement, because we run one business.

23. **Our commitments.** It is our policy to:

    (a) understand what the customer actually requires, in writing, before we start; carry out
        the work in accordance with the specification, the manufacturer's instructions, the
        relevant British and European Standards, Building Regulations and the scheme rules of the
        trade; inspect and test it, and record that we have; and stand behind it;

    (b) provide and maintain safe and healthy working conditions, safe plant, equipment and
        systems of work, and adequate welfare on every site, for the prevention of work-related
        injury and ill health. Our work carries significant risk — principally falls from height,
        fragile surfaces, manual handling, hazardous substances, dust and respirable crystalline
        silica, noise, hand-arm vibration, asbestos in existing buildings, electricity, and work in
        occupied and operationally sensitive premises — and controlling it is a core operational
        objective, not an administrative one;

    (c) eliminate hazards and reduce risks at source, in the order the law requires, with
        protective equipment the last line of defence and never the first, recorded in written
        risk assessments and method statements before work starts;

    (d) protect the environment: prevent pollution of air, land, surface water and groundwater;
        apply the waste hierarchy and discharge our duty of care under section 34 of the
        Environmental Protection Act 1990 in full; reduce the energy, fuel, water and materials we
        use and the waste we generate; source materials responsibly, including timber from legal
        and sustainable sources; and respond promptly and effectively to any incident;

    (e) comply with the law, with our compliance obligations, with the Construction (Design and
        Management) Regulations 2015 in whichever duty-holder role we occupy, with the site rules,
        permits and security requirements of every customer on whose premises we work, and with
        every contractual and scheme requirement we have accepted — and evaluate, at planned
        intervals, whether we do;

    (f) employ and engage only people competent for the work they are given, appoint a competent
        person to advise us on health and safety law, and provide the information, instruction,
        training and supervision every person needs to work well and safely;

    (g) consult our employees & subcontractors on the matters that affect their work, their
        health and safety and the environment, and act on what they tell us. For health and
        safety that consultation is with the non-managerial workers themselves, through the
        mechanism at Part 5 of SESC-IMS-05, and they take part in identifying hazards, setting
        controls, investigating what goes wrong and improving the system;

    (h) require our subcontractors and suppliers to meet the standards we set for ourselves,
        through our Supplier Code of Conduct;

    (i) set measurable objectives for quality, environment and health and safety, resource them,
        and review performance against them at least annually;

    (j) monitor, inspect and audit our work and our system; investigate accidents, incidents,
        near misses, complaints and non-conforming work; correct what we find, find out why it
        happened, and deal with complaints promptly, fairly and without argument; and improve
        continually, using what our records and our customers tell us; and

    (k) communicate openly with our customers, our workforce, our neighbours and regulators, and
        make this policy available to any interested party on request.

24. **Climate change.** We have determined that climate change is a relevant issue to our
    business and to this management system, and that our customers, our regulators and our
    defence supply chain have climate-related requirements of us. We will plan work for the
    weather and the heat our people now face on roofs and in roof voids; reduce the fuel and
    plant emissions we measure under our Carbon Reduction Plan, and obtain the energy data for
    our own premises that we do not yet hold; contain and recover the refrigerants we handle,
    which are the largest direct climate control we operate; and deliver the heat pumps, solar
    photovoltaic systems and energy-efficiency measures that reduce our customers' emissions by
    far more than our own operations emit.

25. **Ethics and culture.** Every record we keep says what actually happened. No certificate,
    inspection, test, training, attendance or accident record is signed for something that was
    not done; concealing defective work, and giving a concession on site without authority, are
    gross misconduct. Every person working for us has the authority, and the duty, to stop work
    that cannot be carried out safely, and no person will suffer any detriment for exercising it
    or for raising a concern in good faith, including through the confidential routes in our
    Whistleblowing (Speak Up) Policy. A mistake that is raised is corrected without blame. We do
    not claim any certification, membership, accreditation or capability we do not hold, and we
    tell customers, insurers and assessors what we do not yet have rather than let them find it.

26. **Scope and application.** This policy covers all work carried out by the Employer, in every
    trade, and applies to everyone working for us: employees & subcontractors, directors and
    officers, consultants and contractors, apprentices and trainees, casual or agency staff and
    any volunteers. It is displayed at our registered office, issued to every employee and
    subcontractor at induction, and available to any interested party on request. The Managing
    Director will review it at least annually, and following any material change in our work,
    our organisation, our customers' requirements, the schemes we are registered with or the law.

27. **Accountability.** Overall and final responsibility for quality, environment and health and
    safety rests with the Managing Director. Day-to-day responsibility rests with the Contracts
    Manager, who is the Quality Representative, the Environmental Manager, the Health and Safety
    Officer and the competent person appointed under regulation 7 of the Management of Health and
    Safety at Work Regulations 1999. The organisation and arrangements by which this policy is
    implemented are set out in SESC-IMS-04 ¶11 and in Parts 2 and 3 of SESC-POL-16, SESC-POL-08
    and SESC-POL-05, which form part of this policy.

28. **A note on certification.** Our management system is structured on the framework of
    ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018. The Employer is not certified to any of
    them, no certification body has assessed us, and we do not claim to be. Any statement that we
    operate a certified system is incorrect and must be withdrawn. This statement is issued, for
    health and safety, as the general statement of policy required by section 2(3) of the Health
    and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974; the organisation and arrangements required by the same
    section remain in SESC-POL-05 Parts 2 and 3.

## Part 5 — Consultation and participation of workers (ISO 45001 clause 5.4)

### 5.1 What is required, and what the Employer has today

34. ISO 45001 requires the Employer to establish and maintain a process for consulting
    non-managerial workers, and for their participation, in developing, planning, implementing,
    evaluating and improving the system; to give the mechanisms, time, training and resources
    for it; to give timely access to clear information about the system; to find and remove the
    barriers to taking part; and to retain records of it operating. The clause names the matters
    on which consultation is emphasised — among them the policy, the objectives, the roles, the
    legal and other requirements, what is monitored, the audit programme and improvement — and
    those in which participation is emphasised — the mechanisms themselves, hazard identification
    and risk assessment, the actions that eliminate hazards and reduce risks, competence needs,
    what is communicated, control measures, and the investigation of incidents and
    nonconformities. The clause map holds it as the clause ISO 45001 auditors test hardest and
    the one small firms fail, because a paragraph saying that workers are consulted is not a
    mechanism and produces no record.

35. **What the Employer has today.** SESC-POL-05 O7 ¶15 states that the Employer consults its
    employees & subcontractors directly under the Health and Safety (Consultation with Employees)
    Regulations 1996, through site briefings, toolbox talks, the daily point-of-work discussion
    and an open route to the Health and Safety Officer and the Managing Director; A3 ¶27 adds the
    confidential routes in SESC-POL-03. SESC-POL-05 A31 ¶172(a) records that **no completed
    toolbox talk or induction attendance record of any kind exists, on any subject, for any
    date.** SESC-FRM-06 Toolbox Talk Record (draft v0.1) is designed to capture what the team
    raised and what was done about it, and its own clause note says that it is one mechanism and
    does not cover clause 5.4 on its own. **No workers' representative has been elected or
    appointed. The four apprentices have not been consulted on anything in the system. No record
    of any consultation exists.** This Part designs the mechanism; it does not claim that it has
    run (Part 6, items (b), (d), (e) and (f)).

36. **Design principles.** The mechanism is built so that a firm of the Employer's size can run
    it every week without a safety committee it does not have: it rides on contact that already
    happens on site; it asks a question every time rather than waiting for someone to volunteer;
    it writes down what was said and what was done; it closes every item with an answer that is
    fed back to the person who raised it; and it reaches the operatives and the apprentices
    directly, not only through their supervisor.

### 5.2 Who is consulted, and who represents them

37. **Direct consultation of all non-managerial workers.** Every non-managerial worker, as
    defined at ¶6, is consulted directly and in person: every directly employed operative,
    **each of the four apprentices individually and in their own right**, and every subcontractor's
    operative for the job they are on. Consultation is in paid time. The 1996 Regulations allow an
    employer to consult employees directly or through representatives of employee safety elected
    by them, and direct consultation is the Employer's position today, but **the Employer has not decided whether to offer the workforce an elected
    representative of employee safety. That is a decision for the Managing Director, recorded in
    the Master Register as D26, and it is not assumed here.** If a representative is elected or
    appointed, this document is reviewed: the representative attends the monthly review at
    ¶38(d), is named on each SESC-FRM-08 record, and may raise items on behalf of others; direct
    consultation of every worker continues alongside, because a representative is an addition to
    it and not a substitute for it.

### 5.3 The mechanism: what happens, how often, and on what

38. The mechanism has five parts. Each produces a record, named in the right-hand column.

    | # | What | Who | How often | On what | Record |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | (a) | **The site toolbox talk.** On every active site, the Site Supervisor delivers a talk and, every time, asks the team what they have seen, what worries them, what is not working and what they would change. ### 5.4 How what is raised gets answered

40. **Every item gets an answer, and the person who raised it is told.** An item raised at a
    toolbox talk is either answered on the day, and the answer written on the SESC-FRM-06, or it
    is carried to SESC-FRM-08 within five working days with an owner and a due date. The owner
    answers within ten working days, or tells the person who raised it when the answer will come
    and why. The answer is given in person at the next toolbox talk on that site — "what we did
    with what you raised" is a standing item on every talk — and the SESC-FRM-08 entry that closes
    the item records the date and how feedback was given. An item is closed only by an answer. "We
    will look into it" is not an answer and does not close an item. A refusal is an answer, if it
    says why.

41. **Where an item goes next.** A hazard or near miss becomes an SESC-FRM-01 and is treated
    under SESC-POL-05 A24. A failure of a control is a nonconformity on SESC-FRM-05. A proposal to
    change a document goes to the Chief Operating Officer as a proposed change under
    SESC-IMS-00 section 6.5. A matter needing spend goes to the Managing Director at ¶38(d). The
    SESC-FRM-08 item records where it went, so that the chain from "a worker said" to "the Employer
    did" can be followed.

42. **How the mechanism is measured**, from the first quarter in which it runs, and reported at the
    management review: the number of items raised per talk and per quarter; the proportion
    answered within ten working days; the proportion closed with feedback recorded; the number of
    items raised by apprentices; and the proportion of active sites with a weekly talk recorded. A
    rising number of items raised is treated as a positive indicator, as a rising near-miss count
    is under SESC-POL-05 A7 ¶45. **No measure has yet been taken** (Part 6, item (k)).

### 5.5 Removing the barriers to taking part

43. The barriers the Employer has identified, and what the mechanism does about each:

    (a) **Fear of reprisal.** Stop-work authority and no detriment (SESC-IMS-04 ¶13; ¶25 of the
        policy). SESC-FRM-01 is anonymous by design, and SESC-POL-03 gives a confidential route.
        An item raised anonymously is answered to everyone at the next talk.

    (b) **Time.** Consultation is in paid time, at the start of the working day on site, and the
        quarterly meeting is held in paid working time. An item is not deferred because the programme is
        tight; the Managing Director has taken the decision at SESC-POL-05 O1 ¶8(f) on every
        conflict between safety and commercial pressure.

    (c) **Understanding and language.** SESC-FRM-06 records whether everyone understood and what
        was done where they did not. Where a worker's first language is not English, the
        Supervisor confirms understanding individually; translation is arranged for a document
        consultation.

    (d) **Inexperience, and the apprentices.** An apprentice is asked by name at every talk and
        attends the quarterly meeting, and nothing raised by an apprentice is answered through the
        apprentice's supervisor alone. The Employer treats the apprentices as the people most
        likely to see a risk that everyone else has stopped noticing, and most likely to say
        nothing unless asked.

    (e) **Subcontractors' workers.** Consulted on the job they are on, through (a), with their
        employer told of anything that bears on its own arrangements.

    (f) **Access to information.** The policy, the RAMS, the objectives once set, the site
        inspection findings and the lessons from incidents are given at (a) and (c) in plain
        language, not on request. SESC-WI-01 tells everyone how to raise a record from a phone.

### 5.6 Why a further form is needed, and why SESC-FRM-06 is not enough

44. SESC-FRM-08 Worker Consultation Record is taken for this mechanism, as draft v0.1 alongside
    this document. SESC-FRM-06 is kept as the record of (a) and is not changed. Four things
    SESC-FRM-06 cannot do, by its own design, are the reason:

    (a) it is tied to a talk, a site and a job number, so the matters ISO 45001 names for
        consultation — the policy, the objectives, the audit programme, the roles, the controls on
        procurement — which belong to no job and no site, have no record;

    (b) it records what the team raised on the day and what will be done, but nothing tracks the
        item to an answer across the weeks that follow, or records that the answer was fed back;
        an item that was "to be looked into" at one talk and never mentioned at the next is
        invisible;

    (c) it does not record which of the clause's consultation or participation matters an item
        serves, so the Employer cannot show an assessor, topic by topic, that each was consulted
        on; and

    (d) participation in a risk assessment review, an incident investigation or the choice of
        PPE happens away from the toolbox talk and has nowhere else to be recorded as
        participation.

45. SESC-FRM-08 records one consultation event, or one answer, per entry: the type of event at
    ¶38(b) to (e), what was consulted on, who was present with the number of non-managerial
    workers and of apprentices, how it was run, each item raised with its own reference, the
    clause matter it serves, the response, owner and due date, where the item went next, and how
    and when feedback was given. A later entry answers or closes an earlier item by reference,
    so the record is append-only and a correction is a new entry (D15). A completed record names
    people and is held in Workspace under SESC-POL-14, never in the repository; the repository
    holds the form and the record metadata only.

### 5.7 When the mechanism starts, and what counts as it operating

    with the "what the team raised" question answered on a live site, which is the first toolbox
