# SESC-IMS-04 · Context of the Organisation

**Clause 4 of ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018 — written once, for all three.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 18 August 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This document is the first artefact of the Integrated Management System. It is issued for
> review by the Chief Operating Officer and approval by the Managing Director. Until the
> Approval block at Part 9 is completed it evidences nothing.
>
> **The Employer holds no ISO certification of any kind and does not claim to.** No
> certification body has ever assessed the Employer. Every statement in this document is
> written to be true on the day it is read, or is marked at Part 8 as a record that does
> not yet exist, with the date by which it will.

---

## Part 1 — Purpose, and why this document exists once rather than three times

1. This document determines the context of the Employer, the interested parties relevant to
   the management system, the scope of that system, and the processes within it. It satisfies
   clause 4 of all three standards to which the Employer is building — ISO 9001:2015, ISO
   14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018.

2. It is written once. Three separate context documents would produce three separate scopes,
   three sets of interested parties and three answers to the same question, which is four
   management systems in one folder and is worse than four folders. The Employer operates one
   business, from one site, with one workforce, and it will operate one management system.

3. Where a requirement applies to only one standard, that is stated in the paragraph and
   tagged in the clause map at Annex A. Nothing is claimed for a standard it does not satisfy.

4. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function
   reserved to the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will
   be performed by the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

5. **Scope of application.** This document applies to all Staff of the Employer, irrespective
   of seniority, tenure and working hours, including all employees & subcontractors, directors
   and officers, consultants and contractors, casual or agency staff, apprentices, trainees,
   homeworkers and fixed-term staff and any volunteers.

## Part 3 — Scope of the Integrated Management System (clause 4.3)

### 3.1 The scope statement

14. **The scope statement, in the form in which it will appear on any certificate:**

    > **The provision of roofing, building fabric, mechanical, electrical and renewable energy
    > installation, refurbishment, maintenance and repair services to commercial, public sector,
    > social housing, defence and domestic customers, including the design of building services
    > and renewable energy systems within the scope of the certification schemes held; carried
    > out from Unit 19 Melbury Business Park, West Melbury, Shaftesbury, Dorset SP7 0AF and at
    > customer premises and Government establishments.**

15. **Roofing is inside the scope, and is named first.** It is named first in the business
    description of every existing policy, it drives the Employer's highest-priority health and
    safety risk, and four operatives are directly employed on roofing work. A scope that omitted
    it would not describe the business. Two consequences follow and are recorded rather than
    avoided:

    (a) The liability policy business description covers roofing undertaken by bona fide
        subcontractors only. That restriction has been verified against the schedule and all
        sixteen endorsements. It is SESC-REG-01 risk B1, it is outside tolerance, and it must be
        resolved by a written mid-term amendment before any assessment. **An auditor reading the
        scope statement and then the insurance schedule finds this on one page.**

    (b) Roofing is the largest trade the Employer carries out for which it holds no third-party
        competent-person scheme registration. NFRC is an application only and **must not be
        claimed anywhere**. This is a genuine feature of the context, not a defect to be hidden:
        it means the management system, rather than a scheme assessment, is the only assurance
        over roofing work, which raises rather than lowers what clause 8 must do.

16. **Sites and boundaries.** One site, Unit 19, comprising office and stores, plus customer
    premises and Government establishments including Boscombe Down. Work carried out by the
    Employer on a customer's site is inside the scope. The customer's own management system,
    and work carried out on that site by others, is outside it.

17. **Functions and activities inside the scope.** Enquiry and contract review; procurement and
    subcontractor control; design and specification within the scope of the schemes held;
    planning, RAMS and CDM duty-holder functions; installation, refurbishment, maintenance and
    repair in all trades named at paragraph 7; inspection, testing, commissioning and handover;
    guarantee and post-completion service; and the supporting functions of competence, plant and
    equipment, waste, and information management insofar as they bear on quality, environment or
    health and safety.

18. **What is outside the scope, and why.** Each of these is a statement about what the
    Employer does not do, verifiable by looking at the business, and not a device for avoiding a
    requirement:

    | Excluded | Basis |
    |---|---|
    | Manufacture of any kind | The Employer manufactures nothing in-house and makes no parts. It has no manufacturing facilities. |
    | Laboratory testing | The Employer operates no laboratory. Test instruments are calibrated externally and traceably. |
    | Licensed asbestos work | The Employer does not carry out licensed asbestos work, does not hold and does not seek a licence. Asbestos management is in scope; licensed removal is not. |
    | Work with significant lead exposure, and work with radioactive sources or radiation generators | Neither is carried out. |
    | Marine, coastal, estuarine and offshore work | None is carried out; the Employer operates no vessel. |
    | Manufacture or import of chemical substances | The Employer is a downstream user under UK REACH and places no substance on the market. |
    | Application of pesticides, herbicides, rodenticides and moss-killing biocides | None is used in the course of the work. Roof moss is removed mechanically or the client is referred to a specialist. |
    | Oil-fired appliance work | Not offered while the OFTEC registration remains lapsed. If the registration is reinstated this row is deleted and the scope statement revised. |
    | Electrostatic protected area work to BS EN 61340-5-1 | The Employer does not handle loose static-sensitive devices and operates no EPA. |
    | Distribution of products | The Employer distributes no products. |

19. **Nothing in the list at paragraph 18 removes a requirement of any standard.** They describe
    activities the Employer does not carry out. **The only clause of any of the three standards
    for which non-applicability could be argued is ISO 9001 clause 8.3, and it is not argued —
    see Part 7.**

### 3.2 What the scope statement is for

20. The scope statement is the first thing an auditor reads and the first thing a customer
    reads on a certificate. It is therefore written to be checkable: every trade named at
    paragraph 14 is a trade for which there is work on the books, an operative or a
    subcontractor who carries it out, and a record of it having been done. Where that ceases to
    be true of a trade, the scope statement is revised — it is not left to describe an ambition.

## Part 4 — Internal and external issues (clause 4.1)

21. The Employer determines the internal and external issues relevant to its purpose and its
    strategic direction, and which affect its ability to achieve the intended results of the
    management system. Those issues are held as data in `registers/issues.yaml`
    (**SESC-REG-06 Issues Register**), reviewed at each management review and whenever a
    trigger at the front matter of this document occurs. This Part states the analysis; the
    register holds the entries, the owners and the dates.

22. **The issues are derived, not invented.** Twenty-six risks were scored, owned and dated in
    SESC-REG-01 Business Risk Register v1.0 on 12 August 2026 and signed by the Managing
    Director. Every issue below traces to one or more of them. Where an issue has no risk behind
    it, that is said.

23. **Risks and opportunities are held in separate columns of the register and are never
    merged.** ISO 9001:2026 separates them and the Employer's register is built that way from
    the first entry, at no cost now and saving a rebuild later.

### 4.1 External issues

24. **Customer and market.**

    (a) Turnover is concentrated in a small number of customers and frameworks, in social
        housing, public sector and defence. The loss of one framework place is material
        (SESC-REG-01 E1).

    (b) Customers increasingly require ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 certification, and the
        Employer holds none. This is the issue that caused this management system to be built
        (SESC-REG-01 C2).

    (c) Defence supply-chain customers apply supplier assurance through JOSCAR, Constructionline,
        SSIP and their own onboarding, all of which test evidence rather than policy
        (SESC-REG-01 C5).

    (d) **Opportunity:** the demand that creates the requirement also creates the reward.
        Constructionline waives the quality module for ISO 9001 and the environmental module for
        ISO 14001, and ISO 45001 satisfies SSIP where SSIP is inside the certification scope.
        One exercise removes three prequalification burdens.

25. **Regulatory and statutory.** Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015; the
    Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the regulations under it; the Building Safety Act
    2022 and Building Regulations Parts L and F; the Environmental Protection Act 1990 duty of
    care and the waste hierarchy; hazardous waste and persistent organic pollutant controls; the
    F-gas regime and its phase-down; UK REACH as a downstream user; UK GDPR and the Data
    Protection Act 2018. **Two statutory items are recorded as not in place and are being
    corrected: the ICO data protection fee, and the verification of the upper-tier waste carrier
    registration.**

26. **Defence and secure-site requirements.** Site rules, permits, inductions, security
    clearance and vetting, restrictions on photography, recording, mobile devices and data
    storage; and, for MOD-adjacent work, Cyber Essentials under DEFCON 658 and the Cyber
    Security Model, which is mandatory at every level with no contract-value threshold. Vetting
    and pass timescales are an operational constraint, not an administrative one
    (SESC-REG-01 H2).

27. **Supply chain and materials.** Copper, cable, insulation, roofing materials, heat pumps,
    inverters and modules are subject to price movement and allocation, and lead times extend
    without notice (SESC-REG-01 E3). A large proportion of the work is delivered by
    subcontractors, and most of the Employer's environmental impact is embodied in the materials
    bought and the waste contractors used.

28. **Labour market.** A national shortage of gas, electrical and heat pump engineers, against
    which a small employer competes on pay and benefits. Four apprentices are in training
    (SESC-REG-01 E5).

29. **Certification and scheme bodies.** Continued registration with NICEIC, Gas Safe, MCS,
    PAS 2030 and F-gas is a condition of offering the work those schemes cover, and each rests
    on one or two named individuals (SESC-REG-01 C4).

30. **Insurance market.** The professional indemnity limit held is £250,000 in the aggregate,
    costs-inclusive, with a retroactive date of 28 April 2025 — low against the design
    responsibility described at Part 7 and below what some customers require
    (SESC-REG-01 B2).

31. **Economic and financial.** Payment terms extended by main contractors, retentions held for
    long periods, and customer financial checks and credit scoring against a company whose last
    filed statutory accounts are for the year ended 31 March 2022 (SESC-REG-01 E2).

### 4.2 Climate change — a relevant issue, and why

32. **The Employer has determined that climate change is a relevant issue to its management
    system, and that the relevant interested parties have climate-related requirements.** This
    determination is made expressly, as required by Amendment 1:2024 to ISO 9001:2015 and
    ISO 45001:2018 and as carried into ISO 14001:2026. **For a building services contractor
    working at height, in southern England, on and near chalk catchments, and in a defence
    supply chain committed to net zero, a "not relevant" conclusion would not be defensible.**
    The determination is substantive, not a sentence added to satisfy the clause:

33. **How climate change affects the Employer — adaptation.**

    (a) **Wind, storm and rain stop roof and external work.** This is already recorded at
        SESC-REG-01 H1 with a likelihood of 5, the highest on the register. Increasing frequency
        and intensity of winter storms shortens working windows, and programmes set without a
        weather contingency turn a weather event into a liquidated-damages event.

    (b) **Heat stress in occupied buildings, in roof voids and on roofs.** A health and safety
        issue for operatives that the existing risk assessments treat as seasonal and will need
        to treat as recurrent. This is an ISO 45001 clause 6.1.2 input, not only an environmental
        one.

    (c) **Surface water and flooding.** The Employer works in catchments draining to Poole
        Harbour, Christchurch Harbour, the Solent and the Channel, on and near chalk aquifers and
        chalk streams. Heavier rainfall events increase both the risk of run-off from sites
        carrying silt, bitumen, oils and cleaning products to surface water drains, and the risk
        of a site or the unit itself flooding.

    (d) **Building fabric failure modes change.** Interstitial condensation, mould and damp
        remediation, and overheating retrofit are growing parts of the work precisely because the
        climate is changing.

34. **How the Employer affects climate change — mitigation.**

    (a) The Employer's own measured emissions are Scope 1 fleet and plant fuel: 21,900 litres
        of diesel over 75,026 business miles in the 2023 baseline year, 54.97 tCO2e, reducing to
        46.07 tCO2e in the 2025 reporting year, a 16.2 per cent reduction. Eight vehicles, seven
        fitted with telematics, and one forklift.

    (b) **Scope 2 is unmeasured.** No metered electricity, gas or water data has ever been held
        for Unit 19 separately from the landlord. SESC-CRP-01 publishes an accurate partial
        figure with a stated completion date rather than an estimate it cannot substantiate, and
        that is the right treatment. It is nonetheless a gap, it is SESC-REG-01 H3, and twelve
        months of data must be obtained from the landlord or the supplier.

    (c) **Refrigerant.** The Employer holds F-gas company certification and handles refrigerants
        whose global warming potential is orders of magnitude above CO2. Containment, recovery
        and leak checking are the single largest direct climate control the Employer operates.

    (d) **Opportunity, and the largest one the Employer has.** The heat pumps, solar
        photovoltaic systems, insulation and energy-efficiency measures the Employer installs
        reduce customers' emissions by far more than the Employer's own operations emit. Decar-
        bonisation policy is the growth driver for the renewables and retrofit side of the
        business. This is recorded as an opportunity in SESC-REG-06, in its own column.

35. **Climate-related requirements imposed on the Employer by others.** PPN 006 Carbon Reduction
    Plans on qualifying public contracts; MOD net-zero commitments flowed down through JOSCAR
    Zero; embodied-carbon and materials disclosure requirements flowed down by main contractors;
    social value and net-zero weightings in tender scoring; Building Regulations Part L; and the
    F-gas phase-down constraining what may be installed. These are recorded against the parties
    that impose them in SESC-REG-05.

### 4.3 Internal issues

36. **Governance and key person.** A single director on whom direction, banking, customer
    relationships and technical decisions all depend, with no second signatory and no documented
    alternate (SESC-REG-01 A1, scored 20). Independence for grievances, Speak Up reports,
    conflicts of interest and appeals involving the Managing Director is achieved only through
    the independent professional adviser, now appointed (A2).

37. **The accuracy of what has already been said to customers.** SESC-REG-01 A3, scored 20,
    records that professional indemnity was declared as £5,000,000 against an actual £250,000,
    that employee numbers were declared five different ways, that supplier environmental
    arrangements and a supply chain compliance process were declared that did not exist, and
    that a data protection compliance statement was made before any data protection
    documentation existed. **This is the reason the honesty convention exists, and it is an
    internal issue of the first order: a management system built on top of inaccurate
    declarations inherits them.**

38. **Records, not documents, are the constraint.** Twenty-one policies exist and all twenty-one
    are signed. What did not exist in the business before 31 August 2026 was a single completed
    toolbox talk attendance record on any subject for any date, a populated calibration register,
    one completed subcontractor assessment, an accident book, a COSHH register that is the
    Employer's own, an environmental aspects register that is the Employer's own, or a
    corrective action log of the Employer's own. **The first quarter in which the Assurance
    Calendar is fully operated is the quarter ending 30 September 2026, and that is the date to
    quote when asked when the management system started working.**

39. **Competence and training records.** Two training matrices existed, the roofing team appeared
    on neither properly, twenty-two qualifications across the site workforce are expired against
    twenty current, one asbestos awareness certificate has lapsed, and no DBS check has been
    completed for anyone (SESC-REG-01 D4). Each carries a dated closure commitment at POL-05 §173.

40. **No independent assurance has ever been obtained.** No internal audit has ever been carried
    out, under any system, for any period. No management review has been minuted since 10 January
    2023. A single trained internal auditor cannot audit their own work in a business this size,
    so two people must be trained and the first cycle bought in for independence.

41. **Inherited material.** Documents adopted whole from other organisations have had to be
    withdrawn — a landscaping firm's aspects register, a fabrication workshop's forty-five COSHH
    assessments, a typhoon emergency plan naming an unconnected company, and a Health and Safety
    Policy 2024 that stated CDM 2015 may not apply to the Employer. **Borrowed material is used
    as structure and never as content, and nothing enters this management system carrying
    another organisation's identity.**

42. **Financial position.** No statutory accounts filed since the year ended 31 March 2022; the
    only accounts in existence show net assets of £6,243; turnover declared three different ways
    (SESC-REG-01 E2). This constrains what the Employer can commit to and how quickly.

43. **Capability the Employer has and should not understate.** A signed, internally consistent
    policy suite of twenty-one documents; trade scheme registrations that already impose and
    verify real competence; an Assurance Calendar with twenty-nine recurring obligations, named
    owners and dates; a scored and owned risk register; measured Scope 1 emissions with a
    verified reduction; and a stated institutional preference for saying what is not true rather
    than concealing it. **A small contractor that can produce those things is ahead of most of
    its peer group, and the honest gap inventories are the reason an assessor can trust the rest.**

## Part 6 — The management system and its processes (clause 4.4)

48. The Employer establishes, implements, maintains and continually improves one Integrated
    Management System, including the processes needed and their interactions, in accordance with
    the requirements of all three standards.

49. **The system is held as structured source, not as a folder of documents.** Documents,
    registers and records are three different objects with opposite lifecycles, and conflating
    them is why small management systems become unusable:

    | Layer | What it holds | Where it lives | How often it changes |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Documents | Policies, procedures, work instructions, form templates | Markdown with YAML front matter, in the `sesc-ims` repository | Monthly. Approved before issue. |
    | Registers | Risks, opportunities, issues, interested parties, aspects and impacts, compliance obligations, assets and calibration, approved suppliers, competence requirements, objectives | YAML in the same repository, so every change diffs and is attributable | Data with a review cycle. **Never a Word table.** |
    | Records | Inspections, toolbox talks, training, calibration, incidents, audits, supplier assessments, management reviews, handovers | An encrypted database, with attachments in object storage. Append-only. | Daily, created by people on site. |

50. **Personal data does not go in the repository.** Health surveillance, face-fit results,
    occupational health, accident detail naming individuals, individual training records,
    DBS and BPSS outcomes, right-to-work scans, disciplinary, grievance and Speak Up reports and
    diversity monitoring data are held in the encrypted database only. Git history is immutable
    and replicated, which is precisely wrong for a UK GDPR erasure request and for special
    category data. The build fails if a file flagged as personal data is committed. **This is
    enforced by the build, not by memory.**

51. **Every document, register and form declares the clauses it satisfies in its own front
    matter.** From that single relation the system derives the audit pack for each standard, a
    live gap report in which any clause with no artefact — or an artefact with no record in
    twelve months — shows red, reverse impact analysis when a document changes, and the answers
    to the next prequalification questionnaire. **Getting ready for an audit becomes a command
    that runs in seconds rather than a project run once a year.**

52. **Approval.** A change to a controlled document is authored and reviewed by the Chief
    Operating Officer and approved by the Managing Director. **Two named humans. No automated
    tool approves, signs or merges a change to a controlled document.**

53. **The processes of the system, and where each is documented,** are set out in SESC-IMS-05
    through SESC-IMS-10 as those documents are written. Until they exist, the arrangements in
    SESC-POL-16 (quality), SESC-POL-05 (health and safety) and SESC-POL-08 (environment) are the
    operative statements, and this document does not claim otherwise.

## Part 7 — Applicability: ISO 9001 clause 8.3 is in scope

54. **The Employer carries out design and development, and ISO 9001 clause 8.3 applies in full.
    It is not excluded and no non-applicability is claimed.**

55. The determination rests on the Employer's own signed documents, not on an inference:

    (a) **SESC-POL-16 Q4 §19 states it without qualification:** *"Where we design or specify —
        falls and drainage to a roof, a heating or electrical system, a renewable installation —
        the design is carried out by a competent person, checked, and recorded."*

    (b) **SESC-POL-16 Annex B provides for design records with a seven-year retention** —
        *"Design and specification records, and sub-contracted design appointments and reviews"*.
        A retention period is not written for a record class that does not arise.

    (c) **SESC-CAP-008 states that design within the scope of the schemes held is carried out in
        house**, and that mechanical services design beyond that scope is placed with a
        sub-contracted designer.

    (d) The Employer's professional indemnity insurer holds a declaration of own-design build at
        eighty per cent of turnover, and professional indemnity cover exists precisely for design
        liability.

56. **There is a contradiction to resolve, and it is recorded rather than resolved by this
    document.** The Employer's standard quotation currently states that the client is entirely
    responsible for their own design requirements, and excludes design responsibility,
    professional indemnity design services, temporary works design and contractor's design
    portion. **Those two positions cannot both be applied.** An assessor comparing SESC-CAP-008
    with the quotation finds the conflict on one page.

57. **The fix is a carve-out, not a choice between them.** The wording proposed on 17 August 2026
    and awaiting the Managing Director's approval is:

    > *"Design responsibility, professional indemnity design services and temporary works design
    > are excluded, other than design carried out within the scope of the certification schemes we
    > hold — heat loss calculation, emitter and heat pump sizing, and MCS solar PV design — for
    > which we accept responsibility and hold professional indemnity cover. All other design,
    > including architectural and product design, is the responsibility of the client or the
    > client's architect or agent."*

58. **Nothing is applied to any customer-facing document until that carve-out is approved or
    replaced.** Until it is, the scope statement at paragraph 14 carries the design element in
    the form the Employer's own capability statements and insurer already describe, because that
    is the position supported by evidence. **This is an open item at Part 8 and it is the single
    decision on which the ISO 9001 scope turns.**

59. **Sub-contracted design remains inside clause 8.3.** Where design is placed with an external
    designer it is controlled under clause 8.4 as well, and the acceptance criteria at
    SESC-POL-16 Q6 §24(c) and (d) — verified competence and registration, and professional
    indemnity checked for limit, basis and whether costs sit inside the limit — are the control.
    **Placing a design with someone else does not place the requirement with them.**

60. **No other clause of any of the three standards is excluded.** ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 admit
    no exclusions, and the Employer claims none under ISO 9001 beyond the applicability of 8.3
    determined above.
