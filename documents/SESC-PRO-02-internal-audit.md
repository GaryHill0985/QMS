# SESC-PRO-02 · Internal Audit

**Clause 9.2 of ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018 — how the Employer finds out,
by its own planned and independent examination, whether the system it has written is the system
it operates, and whether that system works.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 26 October 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This procedure is issued for review by the Chief Operating Officer, for consultation with the
> Employer's non-managerial workers on the audit programme under SESC-IMS-05 Part 5, and for
> approval by the Managing Director. Until the Approval block at Part 9 is completed it evidences
> nothing. **No internal audit has ever been carried out under any system, for any period, and no
> audit programme has operated** (CLAUDE.md §10; SESC-IMS-00 ¶57). The programme at Annex A is
> proposed, not in force.
>
> **The Employer holds no ISO certification of any kind and does not claim to.**

## Part 1 — Purpose, scope and words used

1. This procedure states how internal audits of the Integrated Management System are planned,
   who carries them out, how they are conducted and reported, what happens to what they find, and
   how the results reach the Managing Director. It is the procedure SESC-IMS-00 Part 10 (o) says
   does not exist, and it replaces, on approval, the separate audit statements at SESC-POL-16 Q23
   ¶69, SESC-POL-05 A8 ¶48(c) and SESC-POL-08 E10, which describe three annual audits of three
   documents. There is one system, and it is audited once.

2. **Scope.** Every clause of the three standards to which the Employer is building, every process
   at SESC-IMS-00 ¶19, every site on which the Employer is working during the audit window, and
   Unit 19. It applies to all Staff of the Employer, including all employees & subcontractors, whose
   work is audited or who carry out an audit.

3. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function reserved to
   the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will be performed by
   the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

4. **Words used.** "An audit" is a systematic, independent and documented examination of evidence
   against a criterion. "The criterion" is the standard clause, the Employer's own document, the
   legal or scheme requirement, or the customer requirement the evidence is tested against. "The
   auditee" is the role whose work is examined. "Conformity" means the evidence shows the criterion
   is met; "effectiveness" means the arrangement achieves what it is for. "A finding" is a
   documented result of an audit: a nonconformity, an observation or an opportunity for
   improvement (¶24). "The programme" is the dated plan of audits over a cycle; "an audit plan" is
   the plan for one audit.

5. **What an audit is not.** It is not the weekly site inspection (SESC-FRM-07), which is a
   monitoring activity carried out by the Site Supervisor on his own site; it is not the external
   assessment under SSIP or CHAS, which is somebody else's audit of the Employer; and it is not the
   certification body's audit. All three are inputs to the programme and none replaces it.

## Part 3 — The audit programme (clause 9.2.2)

### 3.1 What the programme must achieve

10. Over each twelve-month cycle the programme covers every clause of the three standards and every
    process at SESC-IMS-00 ¶19 at least once; it covers every site type the Employer works on
    (an occupied domestic property, a social-housing programme, a commercial building, a Government
    establishment) at least once across the cycle; and it is weighted by risk: the processes with
    the highest consequence of failure — work at height, asbestos, electrical isolation, waste
    duty of care, document control of the RAMS — and the areas with a history of findings or with
    records recorded as not yet held are audited earlier and more often.

11. The programme takes account of: the results of previous audits; the state of the inventory of
    records not yet held in every management-system document; open nonconformities on SESC-FRM-05;
    accident, incident and near-miss data in SESC-REG-03; the Business Risk Register SESC-REG-01;
    the significant aspects in SESC-REG-10 and the obligations in SESC-REG-09; changes in the
    business, the law or the schemes; and anything the certification body says in writing it
    expects to see before Stage 2.

12. **The first cycle.** Because no audit has ever been carried out, the first cycle has a second
    purpose: to establish, with evidence, which arrangements described in the signed documents are
    actually operated and which exist on paper. The first-cycle programme at Annex A is therefore
    front-loaded towards the arrangements the Stage 1 assessment is read against, and every audit
    in it tests the records-not-yet-held sections as a criterion in their own right. **The
    programme at Annex A is proposed for the Managing Director's approval and has not run.**

### 3.2 Setting and changing the programme

13. The Chief Operating Officer drafts the programme for the cycle, consults the non-managerial
    workers on it through the quarterly consultation meeting (SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(c) — the audit
    programme is one of the matters ISO 45001 clause 5.4 names for consultation), and puts it to
    the Managing Director for approval. Approved, the audits are entered in SESC-REG-02 with their
    month, scope and auditor. The programme is held as Annex A to this procedure until the portal
    holds it as data; a change to it is a change to this procedure under SESC-PRO-01, or, where it
    is only a date moving within the cycle, a recorded change in SESC-REG-02 with the reason.

14. An audit is added outside the programme when: a major nonconformity, an accident, a dangerous
    occurrence, an enforcement action or a customer complaint indicates an arrangement has failed;
    the Managing Director asks for one; or the certification body asks for evidence in an area the
    programme has not yet reached.

## Part 4 — Carrying out an audit (clause 9.2.1)

### 4.1 Planning one audit

15. Not less than ten working days before the audit the auditor issues an audit plan (Annex B
    part 1) naming: the scope — which processes, clauses, documents, sites and jobs; the criteria —
    the clause text, the Employer's document and paragraph, the legal or scheme requirement; the
    auditee roles; the date, place and time; the records to be made available in advance; and the
    method — document review, interview, observation on site, sampling of records. The plan is
    agreed with the Contracts Manager so that the people and records are there.

16. **Sampling.** The auditor states in the plan how records will be sampled (for example every
    RAMS issued in the last quarter, or three jobs chosen by the auditor on the day) and does not
    accept a sample chosen by the auditee. Where fewer records exist than the sample asks for, every
    record is examined and the report says so — a thin record set is itself a finding about
    operating history, not a reason to pass.

### 4.2 Conducting it

17. The audit opens with a short meeting confirming the plan. The auditor then tests each criterion
    by looking for evidence: a record, an observation, a consistent account from the person doing
    the work. **Evidence is what exists; an assurance that something is done is not evidence.** For
    each criterion the auditor records what was looked at, what was found and the conclusion.
    Where a document says a record exists and it cannot be produced, that is recorded as found,
    whatever the explanation; where a document's own records-not-yet-held section already declares
    the record absent with a date, the finding is that the date has or has not passed.

18. **On site.** The auditor observes work in progress against the RAMS, the permits, the
    environmental controls and the welfare standard; speaks to operatives and apprentices directly
    and in their own right, not only to the supervisor, about what they were briefed, what they
    would do if a control failed and what they have raised; and checks the site documents are the
    current revision. An unsafe condition found during an audit is dealt with at once under the
    stop-work authority (SESC-IMS-04 ¶13) and then recorded.

19. **Worker participation.** The audit plan and the findings that affect site are explained to the
    workers concerned at the closing meeting or the next toolbox talk, and anything they raise is
    entered on SESC-FRM-08 (SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(e)).

### 4.3 Grading what is found

20. Every finding is one of:

    | Grade | Meaning | Goes to |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Major nonconformity** | A requirement of the standard or the Employer's own document is not met and the failure is systemic, or a legal or scheme requirement is breached, or a record the Employer has told anyone it holds does not exist | SESC-FRM-05 at once; the Managing Director the same day |
    | **Minor nonconformity** | A single lapse against a requirement that is otherwise operated | SESC-FRM-05 within five working days |
    | **Observation** | Evidence of a weakness that has not yet produced a nonconformity, or a declared records-not-yet-held item whose date has passed | The audit report; the corrective action log as an improvement item |
    | **Opportunity for improvement** | A way the arrangement could work better | The audit report; SESC-REG-08 where it is a business opportunity |
    | **Conformity** | The criterion is met, with the evidence seen named | The audit report, so that what passed is as traceable as what failed |

21. A nonconformity is written so that it can be acted on: the criterion, the evidence, and the gap
    between them, in that order. "The RAMS was not briefed" is not a finding; "SESC-POL-05 A2 ¶22
    requires the RAMS briefing to be recorded on a signed acceptance register; for job J-nnnn no
    acceptance register exists for the revision 2 RAMS issued on [date]; three operatives on site
    stated they had been briefed verbally" is.

### 4.4 Closing and reporting

22. The audit closes with a meeting at which every finding is read to the auditee, the evidence is
    confirmed, and disagreement, if any, is recorded rather than negotiated away. The written report
    (Annex B part 2) is issued within ten working days of the audit to the Managing Director, the
    Chief Operating Officer and the auditee, takes the next free SESC-REC-nn reference, and is
    indexed in `portal/records-index.yaml` against the clauses it tested. The report states the
    scope and criteria actually covered (which may be narrower than planned, and says so), every
    finding by grade, the overall conclusion on conformity and effectiveness, and the auditor's
    statement of independence under ¶8.

23. Audit reports are retained for five years (SESC-POL-16 Annex B; SESC-POL-05 Annex B;
    SESC-POL-08 Annex B) and are available to the certification body and, on request, to a
    customer. A report that names an individual's conduct is held in the records store under
    SESC-POL-14 with a redacted copy in the repository.

## Part 5 — What happens to findings

24. Every nonconformity is raised on SESC-FRM-05 and handled under SESC-PRO-04: correction,
    cause, corrective action, owner, date, and verification of effectiveness before closure. Every
    observation and opportunity is entered on the corrective action log with an owner and a date,
    or is closed with the reason recorded. The Chief Operating Officer tracks every finding and
    reports open and overdue findings to the Managing Director monthly alongside the corrective
    action log (SESC-POL-05 A8 ¶50; SESC-POL-16 Q20 ¶63(c)).

25. **Verification of closure is done by someone other than the person who did the corrective
    action**, normally the auditor who raised it or the Chief Operating Officer, and is recorded
    with the date, what was examined and the name. A finding closed because time has passed is not
    closed.

26. **To the management review.** The programme's completion against plan, every major
    nonconformity, the pattern of minor nonconformities and observations, the time to close, and
    the auditor's conclusions on effectiveness are a standing input to the management review under
    SESC-PRO-03.

## Part 6 — Measuring the programme

27. From the first audit, and reported at the management review: audits completed against the
    programme; findings by grade and by clause; the proportion of nonconformities closed within
    their due date; the proportion of closures verified by a person independent of the action;
    repeat findings against the same criterion; and the number of records declared not yet held
    that the audits found still absent after their date. **No measure has yet been taken.**
