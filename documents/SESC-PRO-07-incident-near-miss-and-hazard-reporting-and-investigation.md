# SESC-PRO-07 · Incident, Near Miss and Hazard Reporting and Investigation

**The incident part of clause 10.2 of ISO 45001:2018 and ISO 14001:2026 — how an accident, a case
of ill health, a dangerous occurrence, a near miss, a hazard or an environmental incident is
reported the same day, recorded, made safe, reported outside the Employer where the law requires,
investigated to its causes, and turned into a corrective action under SESC-PRO-04.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 26 October 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This procedure is issued for review by the Chief Operating Officer and approval by the Managing
> Director. Until the Approval block at Part 9 is completed, SESC-POL-05 A24 and A25 ¶147 and
> SESC-POL-08 E7 ¶38(e) and ¶39 remain the operative statements, and this procedure changes nothing.
>
> **The Employer holds no ISO certification of any kind and does not claim to.** Every statement
> in this procedure is written to be true on the day it is read, or is marked at Part 8 as a record
> that does not yet exist, with the date by which it will. **No accident book, completed accident
> record, near miss report, investigation report or environmental incident report of the Employer's
> own exists at the date of this draft.** That is not a nil return: it is the absence of the record,
> and the first entry will be the first evidence that this procedure operates.

## Part 1 — Purpose, scope and words used

1. This procedure states how any event that hurt someone, could have hurt someone, harmed the
   environment or could have, is reported, recorded, acted on and learned from. It carries into
   one procedure, without changing them, the arrangements signed at SESC-POL-05 A24 (¶132 to ¶138),
   A25 ¶147, O1(e), O2(g), O3(f), O4(f) and A5 ¶35(h); SESC-POL-08 E7 ¶38(e) and ¶39; SESC-POL-06
   ¶15(c); SESC-POL-12 ¶5(e) and ¶6(f); and SESC-POL-19 ¶49 to ¶53; and it puts the two record
   capture forms drafted for the purpose, SESC-FRM-01 and SESC-FRM-02, and the register SESC-REG-03,
   into one sequence that ends in SESC-PRO-04.

2. **Scope.** Every accident, injury, case of work-related ill health, dangerous occurrence and
   near miss arising from the Employer's work, wherever it happens — on the Employer's own site, on
   a customer's or a Government establishment, at Unit 19, in a vehicle on the Employer's business —
   and whoever it involves: an employee, a subcontractor's person, an agency worker, a customer's
   person, an occupant or a member of the public. Every hazard and unsafe condition observed. Every
   environmental incident and near miss: a spill, leak, discharge, release, fire, dust or fume
   event, or a duty-of-care failure. A product safety concern, hazard or threat raised under
   SESC-POL-19 ¶49. It applies to all Staff of the Employer, including all employees &
   subcontractors.

3. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function reserved to
   the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will be performed by
   the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

4. **Words used.** "The Employer" is SESC Solutions Ltd. "An incident" is any event in the scope at
   ¶2. "A near miss" is an incident in which nobody was hurt and nothing was harmed, but could have
   been. "A hazard" is a condition with the potential to cause an incident, reported before anything
   happens. "The accident book" is the approved means of recording accidents required at
   SESC-REG-09 CO-19, held at the registered office and at every site (SESC-POL-05 ¶133). "The
   register" is SESC-REG-03 Accident, Incident and RIDDOR Register. "RIDDOR" is the Reporting of
   Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (SESC-REG-09 CO-18). "Reportable"
   means an event the Employer must report to the Health and Safety Executive under RIDDOR, to the
   Environment Agency under SESC-POL-08 ¶38(e), or to a site authority, customer or insurer under a
   contract or policy condition.

5. **What this procedure does not do.** It does not decide corrective action, grade the resulting
   nonconformity or close it — SESC-PRO-04 does, from the moment the Health and Safety Officer or
   the Environmental Manager raises the SESC-FRM-05. It does not restate the RIDDOR categories and
   deadlines, which are at SESC-POL-05 ¶134 as signed and apply as written there. It does not hold
   any record naming a person. It does not deal with a counterfeit materiel incident (SESC-POL-17
   sections 5 and 6 and Annex A), a personal data breach (SESC-POL-14 section headed Personal data
   breaches), a sanctions event (SESC-POL-18 section 5) or a modern slavery concern (SESC-POL-10
   section headed If we find something), each of which has its own route; where one of those also
   hurt someone or harmed the environment, this procedure applies as well.

## Part 3 — Reporting: the same day, every time

### 3.1 First actions

7. Whoever is first on the scene makes people safe before anything else: stops the work, removes
   anyone from danger, gives or summons first aid, raises the alarm under the project emergency plan
   (SESC-POL-05 ¶142), and where a spill or release is involved follows the spill sequence at
   SESC-POL-08 ¶38(b) — make yourself safe, stop the source if safe, contain, absorb and collect,
   seal and label the waste, report. Where a suspected asbestos-containing material has been
   disturbed, work stops, the area is vacated and secured, and nobody attempts to clean up
   (SESC-POL-05 ¶113).

8. **Preserving the scene.** Following a serious injury or dangerous occurrence the scene is made
   safe and then left undisturbed, and equipment involved is quarantined, until the HSE and the
   Employer's insurers confirm it may be released (SESC-POL-05 ¶137). Photographs are taken before
   anything is moved, and the Site Supervisor records who was present.

### 3.2 Telling the Employer

9. Every event in the scope at ¶2 is reported to the Site Supervisor and the Health and Safety
   Officer **the same day** (SESC-POL-05 ¶132). A death, a specified injury, a dangerous occurrence,
   a release reaching or threatening a drain, watercourse or unmade ground, or anything involving an
   enforcing authority or a site authority, is reported **immediately, by telephone**, and the
   Health and Safety Officer tells the Managing Director — who is notified of every accident,
   dangerous occurrence, RIDDOR report and environmental incident (SESC-POL-05 ¶8(e); SESC-POL-08
   ¶6) — the same day, which this procedure sets as the time limit. "Same day" is not "when the form is done": the telephone call comes first and the
   record follows.

10. **On a customer or customer-nominated site**, including a Ministry of Defence or other
    Government establishment, the event is reported to the site authority or the principal
    contractor **immediately** and to the Health and Safety Officer the same day, and is entered in
    the Employer's own accident and incident register whether or not the site keeps its own record
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶147), and in the accident book where there was an injury (¶12). On a Government establishment an environmental release is reported to the
    site authority first and its instructions followed (SESC-POL-08 ¶38(e)).

11. **A subcontractor** reports every accident, dangerous occurrence, near miss and case of
    work-related ill health arising on the Employer's work to the Employer the same day, and any
    spill, leak, discharge or environmental incident immediately (SESC-POL-12 ¶5(e), ¶6(f)); the Employer records it on its own forms as if it were its own,
    because to the customer it is.

### 3.3 Making the record

12. The record is made on the form designed for it, from a phone on site where the form is live,
    on the day, by the person who was there, under SESC-WI-01 — plain words, facts not conclusions,
    no blame (SESC-FRM-02 `description` help). The forms are:

    | Event | Form | Who completes it | What it holds |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Near miss; hazard or unsafe condition; environmental near miss; a RAMS that missed something; a product safety concern raised on site | **SESC-FRM-01 Near Miss and Hazard Report** | Anyone; a name is optional and a report with no name is a valid report | Date, time, site, job number, category, what happened or nearly happened, what was done straight away, potential severity, whether the RAMS covered it, a photograph |
    | Injury; work-related ill health; dangerous occurrence; damage; an event involving an enforcing or site authority; an environmental incident that caused harm | **SESC-FRM-02 Accident and Incident Report** | The Site Supervisor or the Health and Safety Officer, the same day | Date, time, site, job number, who was involved by status not name, nature of injury, what happened, immediate action, first aid, whether there were witnesses, whether it looks RIDDOR-reportable, whether an authority attended; the root cause and corrective action fields completed at investigation, not at the scene |
    | Any injury to any person | **The accident book** at the registered office and at every site | The injured person, or someone on their behalf | The particulars Schedule 4 to the Social Security (Claims and Payments) Regulations 1979 requires (SESC-REG-09 CO-19), on a format that complies with data protection requirements (SESC-POL-05 ¶133) |
    | Every event recorded on either form | **SESC-REG-03 Accident, Incident and RIDDOR Register** | Health and Safety Officer | The register of incidents, from which the indicators at SESC-POL-05 A7 are counted |

13. **Witnesses and names.** Names of witnesses are taken separately and are not typed into the
    form (SESC-FRM-02 `witnesses` help). A completed SESC-FRM-02 names or identifies an injured
    person and holds injury detail, which is special category data; it is held in the records store
    under SESC-POL-14 and never enters the repository (SESC-FRM-02 `completed_record`; CLAUDE.md
    §8). A completed SESC-FRM-01 may be anonymous and is treated the same way where it is not.
    What the repository holds is the count, by form type and month, for the staleness test at
    SESC-PRO-01 ¶37.

14. **One event, one record, corrected by a new entry.** A record is never edited or deleted; a
    correction, including the root cause and corrective action added after investigation, is a new
    entry that references the original (SESC-FRM-01 and SESC-FRM-02 `correction_of`; SESC-PRO-01
    ¶28).

15. **A product safety concern** raised by any of the routes at SESC-POL-19 ¶50 — to the Site
    Supervisor, the Contracts Manager, the Managing Director or through Speak Up — is logged by the
    Contracts Manager, acknowledged to the person who raised it within two working days,
    investigated, and the outcome fed back (SESC-POL-19 ¶52). Where it is raised on SESC-FRM-01 the
    form is the log entry; where it is raised in person or anonymously, the Contracts Manager makes
    the SESC-FRM-01 entry so that one register holds every concern.

## Part 4 — Reporting outside the Employer

16. **RIDDOR.** The Health and Safety Officer determines whether an event is reportable under RIDDOR
    and makes the report to the HSE, online at the address SESC-POL-05 ¶135 gives, or by telephone
    for a fatal or specified injury or a dangerous occurrence, within the deadlines at SESC-POL-05
    ¶134(a) to (g), which are not restated here. The Managing Director is notified of every report
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶8(e)), the same day under ¶9. A copy of every report is retained for at least three years
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶135; SESC-REG-09 CO-18). An over-three-day incapacitation is not reportable but is
    recorded (SESC-POL-05 ¶134(c)). A disease diagnosed through health surveillance that is
    reportable is notified under RIDDOR (SESC-POL-05 ¶129). **The report is the Health and Safety
    Officer's to make**; SESC-FRM-02's note that the Managing Director makes it does not match the
    signed policy and is corrected at the form's next version (Part 8(h)).

17. **The Environment Agency.** Any spill or release that reaches, or may reach, a drain,
    watercourse, groundwater or unmade ground, and any actual or suspected pollution of a
    watercourse or groundwater, is reported immediately by the Environmental Manager to the
    Environment Agency incident hotline on the number at SESC-POL-08 ¶38(e), and to the client and,
    where relevant, the site authority (SESC-POL-08 ¶38(e), ¶80(g)). Where a disposal or
    environmental issue arises from a counterfeit materiel incident the same route applies
    (SESC-POL-17 ¶38).

18. **The customer, the principal contractor and the insurers.** The client, the principal
    contractor and the insurers are notified of any reportable event affecting their site or their
    interests, in accordance with the contract and the policy conditions (SESC-POL-05 ¶138). The
    notification states the facts; any statement beyond the facts — of cause, liability or what
    the Employer will do — is the Managing Director's (¶6). SESC-POL-05 ¶138 names no role; this
    procedure allocates the notification to an insurer to the Accounts Manager, on the Managing
    Director's instruction, within the time the policy condition requires, which the Accounts
    Manager reads from the policy, not from memory.

19. **The site authority on a Government establishment.** Any breach of site security or of a
    permit is reported immediately to the site authority and to the Health and Safety Officer
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶165). A drug or alcohol matter on a customer or Government establishment is
    reported to the site authority and, where possession or supply is involved, to the police
    (SESC-POL-06 ¶23).

20. **Post-incident testing.** Following any accident, dangerous occurrence, near miss with serious
    potential, or damage to property or vehicles, where a person's acts or omissions may have
    contributed, the Health and Safety Officer arranges a drug and alcohol test as soon as
    practicable after any necessary medical attention (SESC-POL-06 ¶15(c)). **No testing provider
    is appointed** (SESC-POL-06 ¶14); until one is, a customer-required test is arranged through
    the customer's provider, and a post-incident test cannot otherwise be carried out, which is
    recorded at Part 8(g).

## Part 5 — Investigation

21. **Every accident, dangerous occurrence and significant near miss is investigated**,
    proportionately to its actual and potential severity (SESC-POL-05 ¶136); every environmental
    incident and near miss is investigated proportionately (SESC-POL-08 ¶39); and a near miss or
    hazard that shows a control failed, or that the RAMS did not cover it, is investigated as the
    free lesson it is (SESC-FRM-01 `rams_covered` help). The Health and Safety Officer, or the
    Environmental Manager for an environmental event, leads or allocates the investigation; the
    worker who does the job takes part (SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(e)); and this procedure adds, as its own
    rule, that the investigator is not the person whose decision is under investigation.

22. **What the investigation establishes** is what SESC-POL-05 ¶136 and SESC-PRO-04 ¶14 require, in
    this order: the sequence of events; the immediate causes; the underlying causes — including
    whether the risk assessment, the method statement, the training, the supervision, the equipment
    or the programme contributed; whether the same condition exists elsewhere (SESC-PRO-04 ¶15); and
    the corrective actions, their owners and their target dates. "Operator error" is never a cause
    without the answer to *why* (SESC-PRO-04 ¶14). Where the investigation finds that a risk
    assessment was inadequate, the assessment is reviewed (SESC-POL-05 ¶19); where it finds that
    competence, training or supervision contributed, SESC-PRO-06 ¶19 applies; where a product was
    involved, the Hazard Log is reviewed (SESC-POL-19 ¶48) and, if the product failed or performed
    outside its published characteristics, it is quarantined under SESC-POL-17 ¶31.

23. **Timescale.** The investigation of an accident, dangerous occurrence or significant near miss
    begins the same day it is reported and the findings are reported to the Managing Director
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶136) within the time SESC-PRO-04 ¶13 sets for the grade — five working days for a
    major, fifteen for a minor — unless the HSE or the insurers' hold on the scene prevents it, in
    which case the reason is recorded. An environmental incident reported to the Environment Agency
    is a major.

24. **The record.** The findings are recorded as a new SESC-FRM-02 entry referencing the original,
    with the `root_cause` and `corrective_action` fields completed, and on the SESC-FRM-05 the Health
    and Safety Officer or Environmental Manager raises for it under SESC-PRO-04 ¶7, tagged OH&S or
    environment. From that point the corrective action, its verification and its closure are
    SESC-PRO-04's, and this procedure's record is complete. An investigation report naming people is
    held in the records store under SESC-POL-14 for seven years, longer where a claim is anticipated
    (SESC-POL-05 Annex B; SESC-POL-08 Annex B; SESC-POL-14 Annex C).

25. **Learning.** The lessons are shared with the workforce by toolbox talk, recorded on SESC-FRM-06
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶136; SESC-POL-08 ¶39; SESC-PRO-04 ¶22); the person who raised a near miss, a
    hazard or a concern is told what was found and what changed (SESC-IMS-05 ¶40; SESC-POL-19 ¶52);
    and the incident is fed into the risk assessment library (SESC-POL-05 ¶19), the aspects register
    where environmental (SESC-REG-10 review trigger), the Business Risk Register where it reveals a
    business risk (SESC-PRO-04 ¶17), and the management review (SESC-PRO-03 inputs 5 and 6).

## Part 6 — Counting, and the indicators

26. The Health and Safety Officer maintains the register from every SESC-FRM-01 and SESC-FRM-02, and
    counts from it the lagging and leading indicators at SESC-POL-05 ¶44 and ¶45: RIDDOR-reportable
    injuries, lost-time injuries, minor injuries treated, dangerous occurrences, cases of
    occupational ill health, the accident frequency rate per 100,000 hours worked and days lost;
    and near misses and hazards reported, where **a rising figure is treated as a positive indicator
    of reporting culture** (SESC-POL-05 ¶45; SESC-IMS-06 OB-H5). Hours are taken from the payroll and
    timesheet records (SESC-POL-05 ¶47), and no rate is published until the hours denominator is a
    dated figure (SESC-IMS-04 ¶12). Environmental incidents are counted for SESC-IMS-06 OB-E4.

27. The indicators are reported quarterly to the Managing Director and to the management review
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶43; SESC-PRO-03 input 6), as counts and rates, never names. **No indicator has yet
    been measured or reported** (SESC-POL-05 Annex A, 2.6.4.8).
