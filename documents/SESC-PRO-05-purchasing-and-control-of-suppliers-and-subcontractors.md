# SESC-PRO-05 · Purchasing and Control of Suppliers and Subcontractors

**Clause 8.4 of ISO 9001:2015, clause 8.1.4 of ISO 45001:2018 and the externally-provided part of
clause 8.1 of ISO 14001:2026 — how the Employer decides who it will buy from and who it will let
work under its name, what it tells them, how it checks what they deliver, and how it keeps or drops
them.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 26 October 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This procedure is issued for review by the Chief Operating Officer and approval by the Managing
> Director. Until the Approval block at Part 9 is completed, the arrangements in SESC-POL-16 Q6 and
> Q7, SESC-POL-05 A28, SESC-POL-08 E8, SESC-POL-17 sections 1 to 3 and 9 to 10, SESC-POL-10, SESC-POL-18
> and SESC-POL-19 section 19 remain the operative statements, and this procedure changes nothing.
>
> **The Employer holds no ISO certification of any kind and does not claim to.** Every statement
> in this procedure is written to be true on the day it is read, or is marked at Part 8 as a record
> that does not yet exist, with the date by which it will. **No supplier or subcontractor has yet
> been evaluated under these arrangements, and the register this procedure operates is empty.**

## Part 1 — Purpose, scope and words used

1. This procedure states how the Employer selects, evaluates, instructs, monitors and re-evaluates
   the organisations that supply it, and how it keeps the products those organisations deliver and
   the work they do under its own control. It gathers into one procedure, without changing them,
   the arrangements already signed in SESC-POL-16 Q6 and Q7, SESC-POL-05 A28, SESC-POL-08 E8,
   SESC-POL-17 sections 1 to 3, 9 and 10, SESC-POL-10 paragraphs 15 to 20, SESC-POL-18 section 1
   and SESC-POL-19 section 19, and it operates one register in place of the four lists those
   documents each describe separately (¶9).

2. **Scope.** Every externally provided process, product or service the Employer relies on to
   deliver the work in the scope statement at SESC-IMS-04 ¶14: materials and products bought for
   installation; plant, access equipment and vehicles hired; subcontractors, whether labour-only
   or supplying labour and materials; sub-contracted designers; waste carriers and the facilities
   that receive the Employer's waste; and the professional and support services the system itself
   depends on — calibration, occupational health, drug and alcohol testing, training, the
   accountant and payroll bureau, and information technology. It applies to free-issue material
   supplied by a customer or a principal contractor, which is checked on the same basis as material
   the Employer buys (SESC-POL-17 ¶19(g)). It applies to all Staff of the Employer, including all
   employees & subcontractors, who order, receive, handle or install anything, or who engage or
   supervise anyone.

3. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function reserved to
   the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will be performed by
   the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

4. **Words used.** "The Employer" is SESC Solutions Ltd. "A supplier" is any organisation in the
   scope at ¶2, and "a subcontractor" is a supplier whose people work on the Employer's sites or
   under its name. "The register" is SESC-REG-11 Approved Supplier and Contractor Register.
   "Evaluation" is the assessment recorded on SESC-FRM-04 before a supplier is approved, and
   "re-evaluation" is the same assessment repeated at the interval at ¶37. "Approved" means entered
   on the register with an outcome of approved or approved with conditions on a dated SESC-FRM-04.
   "An approved source" has the meaning at SESC-POL-17 ¶19(a): the original equipment manufacturer,
   a distributor or stockist authorised by that manufacturer, or an established merchant with a
   trade account in the Employer's name. "Criticality" is the effect a supplier's failure would have
   on conforming work, on health and safety or on the environment, graded critical, significant or
   routine at ¶16.

5. **What this procedure does not do.** It does not restate the acceptance criteria, the receipt
   checks, the counterfeit controls, the sanctions screening or the design appointment process:
   each is cited to the signed paragraph that holds it and applies as written there. It does not
   decide what is bought — that is the technical submittal under SESC-POL-19 section 2 and the
   readiness review under SESC-POL-16 Q3. It does not hold any record. It does not override
   SESC-POL-14 where a supplier is a sole trader or processes personal data for the Employer.

## Part 3 — The register (clause 8.4.1; ISO 45001 8.1.4.2)

7. The Employer keeps one register of the organisations it has evaluated and approved to supply
   it, SESC-REG-11 Approved Supplier and Contractor Register. The register is held in the
   repository as data, and every row is an organisation, a category, a criticality, an outcome
   with the SESC-FRM-04 that produced it, the evidence verified with its expiry, any condition,
   and the date of the next re-evaluation. The register is reviewed at least annually by the
   Contracts Manager and the result recorded (SESC-POL-16 ¶23; SESC-POL-17 Annex B).

8. **What the register is not.** It holds no evaluation detail, no insurance certificate and no
   questionnaire response: those are the records behind each row and are held in the records store
   with the SESC-FRM-04. It holds no personal data. A sole trader is an individual, and SESC-POL-14
   applies: a sole trader is entered on the register under a reference of the form `ST-nn` and a
   trading description only, and the identity behind the reference is held in the records store
   with the evaluation, never in the repository (CLAUDE.md §8; SESC-FRM-04 `completed_record`).

9. **Four lists become one.** The signed documents each describe their own list: the Approved
   Contractor Register at SESC-POL-05 ¶159, SESC-POL-08 ¶52 and SESC-POL-10 ¶16; the list of
   approved and preferred suppliers at SESC-POL-16 ¶23; the Supplier Authorisation Record at
   SESC-POL-17 Annex B; and the approved supplier list at SESC-POL-19 section 19 Stage 2. Each is
   empty. On approval of this procedure, SESC-REG-11 is every one of them, and each name is
   recorded in the register as an alias so that a reference in a signed document is understood.
   Until the four documents are amended at their next revision to point at SESC-REG-11, they carry
   four descriptions of one register, and that condition is recorded at Part 8 and in the Master
   Register.

10. **Entry.** A supplier is entered on the register only from a completed SESC-FRM-04 with an
    outcome of approved or approved with conditions, dated and carried out by the Contracts
    Manager, after the Accounts Manager has recorded a clear sanctions screening. There is no
    other route onto the register. A supplier the Employer has used before this procedure is
    approved is not on the register because it has been used; it is on the register when it has
    been evaluated.

11. **Suspension and removal.** A supplier is suspended, pending re-evaluation, on any supplier
    nonconformity graded major under SESC-PRO-04 ¶8, on any incident involving them, on a sanctions
    possible match, and on any request that an incident be kept between the parties (SESC-POL-17
    ¶39). A supplier is removed for repeated failure (SESC-POL-16 ¶25), for a breach of the Supplier
    Code of Conduct of the kind at SESC-POL-12 ¶19, for a confirmed sanctions match (SESC-POL-18
    ¶42), or where exploitation of workers is substantiated and not remediated (SESC-POL-10 ¶28(g)).
    Removal is recorded on the register with the reason and the date, and a removed supplier is not
    re-entered except by a new evaluation and the Managing Director's approval.

## Part 4 — Evaluation and selection (clause 8.4.1; ISO 45001 8.1.4.2; ISO 14001 8.1)

### 4.1 Before any supplier is used

12. **Screening comes first.** Before an account is opened, a subcontract is let or a first order
    is placed, the Accounts Manager screens the supplier, and where ownership is not obvious its
    owners, directors and persons with significant control, against the UK Sanctions List and the
    UN, EU and OFAC consolidated lists, and records the result on the Screening Record at
    SESC-POL-18 Annex A (SESC-POL-18 ¶13, ¶14, ¶17). A possible match stops everything and goes to
    the Managing Director under SESC-POL-18 section 5. The Contracts Manager does not evaluate a
    supplier the Accounts Manager has not cleared.

13. **Legitimacy of source.** A supplier of anything to be installed is an approved source as
    defined at ¶4, or it is not used. Open-market, auction, marketplace, broker, social-media and
    cold-call sources are not used for anything that will be installed in a customer's building;
    any exception requires the Managing Director's prior written approval, recorded on the
    register with the reason (SESC-POL-17 ¶19(a), (b); Annex B "independent"). For the higher-risk
    product categories at SESC-POL-17 ¶20, the source is the manufacturer or an authorised
    distributor and nothing else.

14. **The criteria.** A supplier is approved only when it meets the acceptance criteria that apply
    to its category, as SESC-POL-16 ¶24(a) to (g) states them, read with the questions the other
    signed policies add. The criteria are not restated here; the table says which paragraph holds
    each and which SESC-FRM-04 field records it.

    | Criterion | Signed paragraph | Applies to | SESC-FRM-04 field |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Legitimacy of source — OEM, authorised distributor or merchant with a trade account | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(a); SESC-POL-17 ¶19(a), ¶22(a), Annex B categories | Every supplier of product | `counterfeit` |
    | Ability to supply what is specified, with the manufacturer's documentation, and to trace it to the manufacturer | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(b); SESC-POL-17 ¶48(b) | Every supplier of product | `counterfeit`; `conditions` |
    | Competence and registration, verified rather than asserted | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(c); SESC-POL-05 ¶159; SESC-POL-19 section 19 Stage 2 | Subcontractors; designers | `hs_competence` |
    | Insurance, verified from the certificate — employers' liability and public liability at the limits required; professional indemnity for anyone designing, checked for limit, basis and costs | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(d); SESC-POL-19 section 19 Stage 3 | Subcontractors; designers; plant hire | `insurance_verified` |
    | Health and safety arrangements proportionate to the work — policy, risk assessments and method statements, SSIP or equivalent where required, accident history and enforcement action | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(e); SESC-POL-05 ¶159; SESC-POL-12 section 1 | Subcontractors; plant hire | `hs_competence`; `conditions` |
    | Environmental arrangements — policy, any certification (verified with the body), prosecutions or enforcement in five years, waste carrier registration and facility permits verified on the Environment Agency public register, segregation, timber sourcing | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(f); SESC-POL-08 ¶51(a), (b); SESC-POL-12 section 2 | Subcontractors; waste carriers; plant hire; material suppliers where relevant | `environmental` |

    | Commercial standing and delivery performance | SESC-POL-16 ¶24(g) | Every supplier | `outcome`; `conditions` |
    | Sanctions screening clear | SESC-POL-18 ¶13, ¶14 | Every supplier | recorded on SESC-POL-18 Annex A, referenced in `conditions` |
    | Data processing — a written contract with the Article 28 terms where the supplier processes personal data for the Employer | SESC-POL-14 ¶22 | Accountant and payroll bureau; occupational health; drug and alcohol testing; IT and cloud providers; training providers holding individual records | `conditions` |

15. **Verification, not assertion.** A certification, registration, permit, licence or membership
    claimed by a supplier is accepted only after it has been verified with the issuing body or on
    the public register, and the verification is recorded with the date (SESC-POL-16 ¶24(c);
    SESC-POL-08 ¶51(b); SESC-IMS-00 ¶49). An ISO certificate is checked on the certification
    body's register and the body's accreditation on the UKAS directory (CLAUDE.md §12.5); an
    ISO/IEC 27001:2013 certificate is treated as no certificate, the 2013 edition's transition
    having expired on 31 October 2025 — a project rule at CLAUDE.md §12.4, not a signed policy,
    recorded here so that it is applied in supplier assessment. An insurance certificate is read for the insurer, the policy
    number, the period, the limit and, for professional indemnity, the basis — not for its existence.

### 4.2 Criticality, and the type and extent of control (clause 8.4.2)

16. The control applied to a supplier is proportionate to the effect its failure would have on
    conforming work, on the health and safety of workers and others, and on the environment
    (SESC-POL-08 ¶51(c); SESC-POL-17 ¶49). The Contracts Manager assigns one of three grades on
    SESC-FRM-04 (`criticality`), and the grade sets the depth of evaluation, the controls on the
    work and the re-evaluation interval:

    | Grade | Who falls in it | Evaluation depth | Controls on the work | Re-evaluation |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | **Critical** | Subcontractors carrying out work in the Tier 1 categories at SESC-POL-19 ¶91 or with a significant environmental aspect at SESC-REG-10; labour-only subcontractors; labour providers; sub-contracted designers; waste carriers and hazardous waste carriers; suppliers in the higher-risk product categories at SESC-POL-17 ¶20; plant hire for lifting and powered access | The full criteria at ¶14, every registration and certificate verified, insurance read from the certificate, references where the work is new to the Employer | RAMS reviewed and approved before mobilisation; competence of every person evidenced before site; induction; monitoring at every weekly inspection; full traceability under SESC-POL-17 section 3; hold points under the Inspection and Test Plan | Every two years, and immediately after any incident or major nonconformity involving them (SESC-POL-08 ¶51(f)) |
    | **Significant** | Subcontractors in the Tier 2 categories; general plant hire; suppliers of specified products outside the higher-risk categories; professional services the system depends on (calibration, occupational health, drug and alcohol testing, training) | The criteria at ¶14 that apply; insurance and registration verified | Receipt checks under ¶26; monitoring through performance under ¶35; a written contract where SESC-POL-14 ¶22 requires one | Every two years |
    | **Routine** | Established national and regional builders', electrical, plumbing and roofing merchants with a trade account in the Employer's name; stationery, consumables and services with no effect on the work | Legitimacy of source and trade account confirmed; Supplier Code of Conduct issued; sanctions screening; no audit of a national merchant is claimed (SESC-POL-17 ¶49) | Receipt checks under ¶26 | Annual review of the register row; re-evaluation only on failure |

17. **The grade may be raised, never lowered, by a customer's requirement.** Where a customer, a
    principal contractor or a Government establishment imposes its own supplier requirements —
    the QinetiQ Supplier Code of Conduct, a security vetting condition, a traceability format —
    those requirements apply in addition and are recorded in the row's conditions (SESC-POL-12 ¶3;
    SESC-POL-17 ¶27).

### 4.3 Outsourced processes (ISO 45001 8.1.4.3)

18. Where the Employer has a process performed by someone else that the standards or its own
    documents require it to control, the process remains the Employer's and is controlled through
    the terms of the engagement, the evidence required back, and the review of what comes back.
    The outsourced processes, and where each is controlled, are:

    | Process | Who performs it | Controlled by |

    | Design in the categories at SESC-POL-19 ¶88 | Sub-contracted designers | SESC-POL-19 section 19, Stages 1 to 8 — brief, competence, professional indemnity, written appointment, design risk assessment, independent review and sign-off, change control, verification; the design element identified at contract review and referred to the Managing Director (SESC-POL-19 ¶90(d)) |
    | Calibration of test and measuring equipment | External calibration provider working to national standards | SESC-POL-16 ¶36(c): certificate retained and referenced on the equipment register |
    | Health surveillance and occupational health | Occupational health provider — **not appointed** (SESC-POL-05 ¶125) | SESC-POL-05 A22; clinical records held by the provider; SESC-POL-14 ¶22 contract |
    | Drug and alcohol testing | Independent testing provider — **not appointed** (SESC-POL-06 ¶14) | SESC-POL-06 section headed Testing; chain of custody; SESC-POL-14 ¶22 contract |
    | External health and safety advice and periodic independent audit | Retained external adviser (SESC-POL-05 O6) | SESC-POL-05 ¶13, ¶117: a recognised occupational health and safety qualification and IOSH or equivalent membership at an appropriate grade required; an engagement letter — **not held** (SESC-POL-05 Annex C); the independence allocation at SESC-PRO-02 ¶8 |
    | Internal audit, where an external auditor is appointed | As decided at D3 | SESC-PRO-02 |
    | Payroll, accounts and pension administration | Accountant and payroll bureau | SESC-POL-14 ¶21, ¶22, ¶29 |
    | Information technology and cloud services | Managed IT service provider; Google Workspace; Xero | SESC-POL-13; SESC-POL-14 ¶27 to ¶29 |

19. An outsourced process is never a reason to have no record. The record the process produces —
    a calibration certificate, a signed-off design, a fitness-for-work outcome, a test result, an
    audit report — is the Employer's record, is held under SESC-PRO-01 Part 4, and is what the
    clause is evidenced by.

## Part 5 — What the Employer tells its suppliers (clause 8.4.3)

20. Every order and every subcontract states what is required, and the requirement follows the
    goods or the work rather than stopping at the first tier. The information given is:

    (a) **On every purchase order** — the manufacturer, the manufacturer's product reference, the
        quantity, any standard, certification, scheme listing or declaration required, the project,
        and the requirement that the goods be new, unused and supplied with the manufacturer's
        documentation; never a generic description where a product has been specified (SESC-POL-16
        ¶27; SESC-POL-17 ¶19(e), ¶25(a)). Where a customer requires traceability beyond
        SESC-POL-17 section 3, that requirement is passed to the supplier on the order (SESC-POL-17
        ¶27). Where the product is one at SESC-POL-16 ¶59 or SESC-POL-08 ¶48(c), the order asks
        the substance-of-very-high-concern and PFAS questions and the answer is recorded.

    (b) **In every subcontract and order, by incorporation** — the Supplier Code of Conduct
        SESC-POL-12, the Employee Code of Business Conduct SESC-POL-01, the Health and Safety Policy
        and Arrangements Manual SESC-POL-05, the Drug and Alcohol Policy SESC-POL-06 and the
        Whistleblowing (Speak Up) Policy SESC-POL-03, issued to the subcontractor at engagement
        (SESC-POL-05 ¶162; SESC-POL-12 ¶3); the counterfeit materiel, traceability, quarantine,
        packaging-disposal and reporting requirements of SESC-POL-17, by reference (SESC-POL-17
        ¶48(a)); the environmental requirements of SESC-POL-08 and the site environmental rules
        (SESC-POL-08 ¶51(d)); and the requirement to flow each of these down to the subcontractor's
        own suppliers (SESC-POL-12 ¶2; SESC-POL-17 ¶48(c)).

    (c) **Before a subcontractor mobilises** — the scope, drawings and specification at the current
        revision; the hazards of the site and the customer's rules; the requirement to submit a
        risk assessment and method statement for the Contracts Manager's approval and to provide
        evidence of the competence of every person attending (SESC-POL-05 ¶160; SESC-POL-12
        ¶5(a), (b)); the induction; and the arrangements for reporting accidents, incidents, near
        misses to the Employer the same day and any environmental incident immediately (SESC-POL-12
        ¶5(e), ¶6(f); SESC-PRO-07).

    (d) **To a sub-contracted designer** — the written brief at SESC-POL-19 section 19 Stage 1 and
        the appointment at Stage 4.

    (e) **On the Employer's conduct towards the supplier** — how the Employer will evaluate and
        monitor them, what it will verify, and that a shortcoming will be raised with them and a
        corrective action agreed (SESC-POL-12 ¶17, ¶18); and the Speak Up route, open to the
        supplier's own workers (SESC-POL-12 ¶11, ¶15).

    (f) **On carbon and emissions data** — the request for the energy, fuel and material data the
        Carbon Reduction Plan needs, and for the supplier's own Carbon Reduction Plan or an honest
        date for one where SESC-POL-12 ¶16 applies.

21. **The terms do not yet carry the Code.** SESC-REG-05 IP-12 records that the Supplier Code of
    Conduct clause has not been inserted into the purchase-order terms, that three sets of terms
    exist in two forms, and that standard terms of subcontract are absent. Until the Managing
    Director has decided which terms go out and the clause is in them, the incorporation at ¶20(b)
    is achieved by issuing the documents with the order and obtaining the acknowledgement at
    SESC-POL-12 section 9, and nothing in this procedure says otherwise. The position is at
    Part 8(d) and (e).

## Part 6 — Purchasing, receipt and the work on site

### 6.1 Ordering

22. An order is raised only against an approved source on the register, or under the Managing
    Director's recorded approval at ¶13. The person raising the order checks the row: that the
    supplier is approved for the category being bought, that no condition on the row is breached by
    the order, and that the row is not suspended.

23. A product is ordered only after the technical submittal for it has been approved in writing
    where SESC-POL-19 ¶21 requires one, and a specified product is never substituted without the
    written approval at SESC-POL-19 ¶22 and ¶23 (SESC-POL-17 ¶19(f)). A price materially below the
    market, or stock offered at short notice of a product on long lead time elsewhere, is escalated
    to the Contracts Manager before ordering, not treated as a commercial win (SESC-POL-17 ¶19(c),
    (d)).

24. **Returned, surplus, reclaimed and second-hand goods** are not bought for installation. The
    one exception is the customer's written request or agreement, the Managing Director's approval,
    a written risk agreement before purchase, verification as far as SESC-POL-17 section 4 permits,
    and identification in the traceability record and the handover pack — all five, as SESC-POL-17
    ¶52 and ¶53 state them. A purchase order is not a risk agreement.

25. Spares and replacement parts for an appliance, boiler, heat pump, inverter or control system
    are the manufacturer's own, obtained through the manufacturer's parts channel (SESC-POL-17
    ¶22(b)). Where a component cannot be obtained through an authorised channel, the Contracts
    Manager refers it to the Managing Director under SESC-POL-17 ¶23 and the decision is recorded.

### 6.2 Receipt and verification

26. Before goods are accepted into use, the person receiving them carries out and records the
    checks at SESC-POL-16 ¶27(a) to (f) — identity and quantity, condition, marking, documentation,
    traceability data, counterfeit indicators — read with the receipt examination at SESC-POL-17
    ¶29 and the conformity checks at SESC-POL-19 ¶34(a) to (c). Goods delivered direct to site are
    checked on the same basis by the Site Supervisor and the check recorded in the site diary
    (SESC-POL-16 ¶28). Batch, lot and serial numbers are recorded and the label retained or
    photographed, and the identification on packaging is captured before the packaging is destroyed
    (SESC-POL-17 ¶25(b), ¶45(a)).

27. Goods failing any check are not put into use: they are marked, physically separated, recorded as
    a nonconformity on SESC-FRM-05 tagged `supplier` under SESC-PRO-04, and returned or quarantined
    (SESC-POL-16 ¶28). Anything suspected of being counterfeit or misrepresented is quarantined,
    never returned to the supplier, and handled under SESC-POL-17 section 5 and reported under
    section 6. A product without its declaration of conformity, incorporation or performance is not
    installed (SESC-POL-19 ¶31).

28. Material stored pending use is stored under SESC-POL-16 ¶29 to ¶31, and shelf-life items are
    date-marked and rotated under SESC-POL-16 ¶30.

### 6.3 Subcontractors on site (ISO 45001 8.1.4.2)

29. Before a subcontractor mobilises, the Contracts Manager confirms that the subcontractor is on
    the register as approved for that category and that the row is not suspended; reviews and
    approves the subcontractor's risk assessment and method statement; and holds evidence of the
    competence of every person who will attend, checked against the requirements for the role at
    SESC-REG-12 (SESC-POL-05 ¶160; SESC-POL-16 ¶17(d); SESC-PRO-06). Where the subcontractor's RAMS
    is integrated into the Employer's own, the Employer's RAMS governs and the subcontractor's
    people are briefed on it and sign the acceptance register (SESC-POL-05 ¶22).

30. Every subcontractor's person attends the Employer's induction before starting work and is
    recorded on SESC-FRM-03, and attends the toolbox talks on the site and is recorded on
    SESC-FRM-06 (SESC-POL-05 ¶28, ¶160; SESC-PRO-06). The subcontractor's people have the same
    authority to stop unsafe work, the same welfare, the same reporting routes and the same Speak Up
    route as the Employer's own (SESC-POL-10 ¶26(c); SESC-POL-12 ¶5(d), ¶15; SESC-IMS-04 ¶13).

31. The hazards and controls of the subcontractor's work are coordinated with the Employer's own
    and with other trades through the RAMS review, the readiness review and the project meetings at
    SESC-POL-16 ¶64, and the subcontractor's residual risks are carried into the construction phase
    plan where the Employer is Principal Contractor (SESC-POL-05 ¶150).

32. **Monitoring.** Subcontractor performance on site — health and safety, environmental and
    workmanship — is monitored at the weekly site inspection on SESC-FRM-07 and at the in-process
    checks, and a shortcoming is raised with the subcontractor, recorded, and where it is a
    nonconformity recorded on SESC-FRM-05 (SESC-POL-05 ¶161; SESC-POL-08 ¶51(e); SESC-POL-12 ¶18).
    A subcontractor's person who breaches SESC-POL-05 may be removed from site at once, and the
    engagement suspended or terminated, with the customer notified (SESC-POL-05 ¶36; SESC-POL-06
    ¶22; SESC-POL-12 ¶19).

33. **A subcontractor supplying material** supplies it under the same traceability information as
    the Employer's own purchases, as a condition of the engagement (SESC-POL-16 ¶34; SESC-POL-17
    ¶19(h)); and a subcontractor working on the Employer's site disposes of packaging under
    SESC-POL-17 section 8, which is included in their induction (SESC-POL-17 ¶48(e)).

### 6.4 Waste carriers and facilities

34. Before any waste contractor is used, the Contracts Manager as Environmental Manager obtains and
    records the contractor's waste carrier registration and the environmental permit or exemption
    of the receiving facility, verifies both on the Environment Agency public register, and
    re-verifies them annually; a contractor whose registration or permit cannot be verified is not
    used (SESC-POL-08 ¶34(b); SESC-POL-12 ¶6(a)). Construction and demolition waste is outside the
    own-waste exemption, so a carrier of it holds an upper-tier registration (SESC-REG-09 CO-43;
    SESC-FRM-04 `environmental` help text). The verification is the Employer's record, as SESC-REC-05
    is for the Employer's own registration, and the facility's recovery rate is obtained where
    segregation on site is impracticable (SESC-POL-08 ¶33).

## Part 7 — Monitoring, re-evaluation and the review of the register

35. **Performance** is monitored on delivery accuracy, the quality of what is supplied, the
    documentation supplied with it, the number of nonconformities attributable to the supplier on
    the SESC-FRM-05 log, delivery against programme, and how the supplier behaves when something goes
    wrong (SESC-POL-16 ¶24(g), ¶25). For a subcontractor the site inspection and in-process records
    at ¶32 are the source. The Contracts Manager reports supplier performance and open supplier
    nonconformities to the Managing Director monthly with the SESC-PRO-04 ¶20 report, and to the
    management review as input 4 (SESC-PRO-03 ¶11).

36. **A supplier nonconformity** is raised on SESC-FRM-05 tagged `supplier`, graded, investigated
    and closed under SESC-PRO-04. A major grade suspends the row (¶11). The investigation asks
    whether the Employer's own ordering, specification or supervision contributed before it concludes
    that the supplier did (SESC-PRO-04 ¶14).

37. **Re-evaluation** is carried out on SESC-FRM-04 at the interval for the grade at ¶16 — two
    years for critical and significant suppliers (SESC-POL-08 ¶51(f); SESC-POL-10 ¶21; SESC-POL-12
    ¶17), the annual row review for routine — and immediately after any incident involving the
    supplier, any major nonconformity, any change of ownership, name or bank details (which also
    triggers a sanctions re-screen under SESC-POL-18 ¶13), and any lapse of a registration,
    certificate or insurance the approval depended on. The re-evaluation date on the row is what
    makes it happen; a row with no date is a defect in the register.

38. **The annual review of the register** by the Contracts Manager, recorded, confirms every row's
    category and grade, checks every registration, certificate and insurance expiry against the
    public registers and the certificates held, confirms that every supplier actually used in the
    year is on the register and raises a nonconformity for any that is not, re-screens every live
    supplier against the sanctions lists through the Accounts Manager (SESC-POL-18 ¶13, "When we
    screen" (b)), and
    reviews the higher-risk product categories at SESC-POL-17 ¶20 and the higher-risk goods at
    SESC-POL-10 ¶19. The result goes to the management review.
