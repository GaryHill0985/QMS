# SESC-PRO-06 · Training, Competence and Awareness

**Clauses 7.2 and 7.3 of ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2026 and ISO 45001:2018 — how the Employer
decides what competence each role needs, makes sure each person has it before they are given the
work, keeps the evidence, and makes sure everyone working under its control knows what the system
expects of them and why.**

> **Status: DRAFT, version 0.1, 26 October 2026. Not approved and not issued.**
> This procedure is issued for review by the Chief Operating Officer and approval by the Managing
> Director. Until the Approval block at Part 8 is completed, SESC-POL-05 A4 and SESC-POL-16 Q5
> remain the operative statements of competence management, and this procedure changes nothing.
>
> **The Employer holds no ISO certification of any kind and does not claim to.** Every statement
> in this procedure is written to be true on the day it is read, or is marked at Part 7 as a record
> that does not yet exist, with the date by which it will. **At the last signed count, 16 August
> 2026, the Training Matrix recorded twenty-two expired qualifications against twenty current, and
> no signed induction or toolbox talk attendance record existed for any date.** Those are the facts
> this procedure starts from.

## Part 1 — Purpose, scope and words used

1. This procedure states how the Employer determines the competence each role needs, ensures each
   person is competent on the basis of education, training or experience before they are given the
   work, takes action where they are not, evaluates whether that action worked, keeps the evidence,
   and makes every person working under its control aware of the policies, the objectives, their
   own contribution and the consequences of not conforming. It carries into one procedure, without
   changing them, the arrangements signed at SESC-POL-05 A4 (¶28 to ¶32), O2(c), O3 and A31 ¶173;
   SESC-POL-16 Q5 ¶22; SESC-POL-08 ¶70, ¶88 and ¶90; SESC-POL-17 sections 11 and 12; SESC-POL-18
   section 3; SESC-POL-19 section 3 and ¶95; SESC-POL-14 ¶37 and ¶38; SESC-POL-10 ¶14(a) and
   ¶26(a); and SESC-POL-06 ¶32; and it adds the register of role-based competence requirements,
   SESC-REG-12, that SESC-IMS-00 ¶36 says will be held in the repository once written.

2. **Scope.** Every person doing work under the Employer's control that affects the quality of the
   work, health and safety, or environmental performance: the Managing Director and the Chief
   Operating Officer; the Contracts Manager in each of the appointments at SESC-IMS-04 ¶11; the
   Accounts Manager; Site Supervisors; every operative in every trade in the scope statement at
   SESC-IMS-04 ¶14, including mould and damp remediation; the apprentices; drivers; and every
   subcontractor's person who attends the Employer's sites (SESC-POL-05 ¶160; SESC-POL-12 ¶5(b)).
   It applies to all Staff of the Employer, including all employees & subcontractors.

3. **The sole-director clause.** Where the Employer has only one director, any function reserved to
   the Board which requires independence from a person involved in the matter will be performed by
   the independent professional adviser named in the Approval block.

4. **Words used.** "The Employer" is SESC Solutions Ltd. "Competence" is the ability to apply
   knowledge and skill to achieve the intended result, shown by evidence, not by attendance alone.
   "A competence requirement" is a qualification, card, registration, assessment, briefing or
   demonstrated ability that a role must hold before a person in that role is given the work; the
   requirements are at SESC-REG-12. "The Training Matrix" is the record, maintained by the Health
   and Safety Officer, of every qualification held by every person, with its issue and expiry date
   and the evidence held (SESC-POL-05 ¶29); it names people, is personal data, and lives in the
   records store under SESC-POL-14, never in the repository (CLAUDE.md §8). "SESC-FRM-03" is the
   Training and Competence Record, the record of one person completing one training, qualification,
   induction or briefing on one date. "Currency" is whether a requirement that expires is in date.
   "Stand-down" is the removal of a person from the activity a lapsed requirement covers, not from
   work (SESC-POL-05 ¶30).

5. **What this procedure does not do.** It does not set the competence requirements — SESC-REG-12
   does, from the signed documents and the scheme rules it cites. It does not state what any
   awarding body, scheme or regulation requires from memory: a requirement, an interval or a
   qualification is read off the issuing body's own documentation and recorded in SESC-REG-12
   with the source (CLAUDE.md §2). It does not hold any record naming a person. It does not
   operate the consultation mechanism (SESC-IMS-05 Part 5): the worker's part in deciding what
   training a job needs is cited at ¶12 and is claimed by SESC-IMS-05 under 5.4, not here. It does not override
   SESC-POL-14, which governs every individual record this procedure causes to exist.

## Part 3 — Determining the competence needed (clause 7.2)

7. **By role.** The competence a role needs is set out in SESC-REG-12 Competence Requirements
   Register: for each role at SESC-IMS-04 ¶11 and each trade at SESC-IMS-04 ¶14, every
   qualification, card, registration, assessment, briefing or demonstrated ability the signed
   documents and the scheme rules require, with the source that requires it, the evidence that
   shows it, the renewal interval where one applies, and who verifies it. A requirement is in the
   register only where a signed document, a scheme standard or a legal instrument in SESC-REG-09
   requires it; the register does not invent requirements and does not state an interval from
   memory.

8. **By job.** The readiness review before work starts on any site confirms that the operatives
   allocated are competent and currently certificated for the work, and that subcontractors are on
   SESC-REG-11 (SESC-POL-16 ¶17(d)). The risk assessment and method statement for the job states
   any competence the job needs beyond the role — a permit, a specific plant category, a
   manufacturer's product training on which the certification or the warranty depends (SESC-POL-19
   ¶26(d); SESC-POL-17 ¶22(b), (c) for the parts and ancillaries the certification depends on) — and the Contracts Manager checks it before allocation. Where the
   work involves a product type for which a person's training or certification has lapsed, that
   person is not given it (SESC-POL-19 ¶27).

9. **By the system.** The people who operate the management system itself need competence in it:
   the Contracts Manager's appointments as Quality Representative, Health and Safety Officer,
   Environmental Manager and regulation 7 competent person; the Accounts Manager's as Data
   Protection Lead; the Chief Operating Officer's as document controller; the internal auditor's
   under SESC-PRO-02. Those requirements are in SESC-REG-12 with the rest. **No audit qualification
   is claimed for anyone** (SESC-PRO-02 ¶7), and the competence of the retained external adviser
   is recorded at SESC-POL-05 Annex C, read off the certificate, and is his, not the Employer's.

10. **Apprentices and young workers.** An apprentice is given only work within their competence
    and their physical and psychological capacity, under a specific risk assessment made before
    they start, with a named mentor, restricted hours and closer supervision (SESC-POL-10 ¶26(a);
    SESC-REG-09 CO-05). The competence an apprentice holds at each stage of their programme is
    recorded on the Training Matrix like anyone else's, and the apprentice is asked by name what
    training they need at every consultation (SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(a), ¶43(d)).

11. **Subcontractors' people.** Before a subcontractor's person attends site, the Contracts
    Manager holds evidence of that person's competence for the work, checked against SESC-REG-12
    for the role, and the person attends the Employer's induction (SESC-POL-05 ¶160; SESC-POL-12
    ¶5(b); SESC-PRO-05 ¶29). The subcontractor's records are the subcontractor's; what the
    Employer records is that it checked, what it saw, and the date.

12. **Workers decide with the Employer what training the job needs.** A worker who does the job
    takes part in determining what training the job needs, and who took part and what they changed
    is recorded on SESC-FRM-08 (SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(e)). That participation is part of the mechanism
    claimed by SESC-IMS-05 under ISO 45001 5.4; this procedure points at it and does not claim it.

## Part 4 — Acquiring competence, and making people aware (clauses 7.2 and 7.3)

### 4.1 Induction

13. Every employee and every subcontractor's person receives a documented induction before starting
    work, signed by the individual and countersigned by the inductor, and the record is retained on
    the personnel file (SESC-POL-05 ¶28). The induction is delivered by the Contracts Manager or a
    Site Supervisor and covers, in one session or in the site-specific part on the day:

    (a) the site rules, emergency and evacuation arrangements, first aid, fire, welfare, accident
        and near miss reporting, work at height, roofing, electrical safety, site traffic, permits,
        hot works, asbestos, housekeeping, PPE issue and the site-specific hazards of the project
        (SESC-POL-05 ¶28);

    (b) the environmental requirements — waste and duty of care, spill response, dust and noise,
        protected species, energy and fuel — and the significant aspects at SESC-REG-10 once
        confirmed (SESC-POL-08 ¶29(c), ¶70, ¶88; SESC-REG-10 significance method);

    (c) counterfeit materiel — what it is, the approved-sources rule, what to look for, and the
        duty to stop and report (SESC-POL-17 ¶55); product safety — installing only approved
        products, checking marking and documentation, following the manufacturer's instructions,
        the Hazard Log hazards for the work, and the stop-work authority (SESC-POL-19 ¶26(a));

    (d) financial sanctions — the red flags and the duty to escalate (SESC-POL-18 ¶24); modern
        slavery — the indicators and the reporting routes (SESC-POL-10 ¶14(a)); data protection —
        the principles, recognising a rights request and reporting a breach (SESC-POL-14 ¶37);

    (e) the policies issued at induction — the Health and Safety, Environmental and Quality policy
        statements and, once signed, the integrated statement at SESC-IMS-05 Part 3, recorded on
        SESC-FRM-03 (SESC-IMS-05 ¶31); SESC-POL-01, SESC-POL-03, SESC-POL-05, SESC-POL-06 and
        SESC-POL-12 for a subcontractor (SESC-POL-05 ¶162; SESC-POL-06 ¶32);

    (f) the objectives relevant to the person's work, their contribution to them, and the
        consequences of not conforming — the gross misconduct list at SESC-POL-05 ¶35 and
        SESC-POL-16 ¶76 stated plainly;

    (g) the authority and the duty to stop work that cannot be carried out safely, which sits above
        every other statement in the system (SESC-IMS-04 ¶13; SESC-POL-05 ¶4), and the Speak Up
        route (SESC-POL-03);

    (h) how to log a record on site — SESC-WI-01, given as the first toolbox talk once the forms
        are live (SESC-IMS-00 ¶37); and

    (i) the person's qualifications and cards, verified against the issuing body and entered on the
        Training Matrix; any medical condition relevant to safety-critical work and occupational
        health history, taken under SESC-POL-05 ¶28 and ¶126(a) and held as health data under
        SESC-POL-14 ¶13, never on the SESC-FRM-03 and never in the repository.

14. The induction record is the first SESC-FRM-03 entry for the person, of type *Induction*, and
    the signed sheet is held on the personnel file. **No signed induction record exists for any
    date** (SESC-POL-05 ¶172(a)); recording was committed to commence on 31 August 2026 and is at
    Part 7(a).

### 4.2 Toolbox talks and briefings

15. Toolbox talks are delivered at least monthly on the programme at SESC-POL-05 ¶25 and ¶26, and
    weekly on every active site as the consultation talk at SESC-IMS-05 ¶38(a), and after any
    relevant incident, change in law or lesson learned (SESC-POL-05 ¶24). Attendance is recorded on
    SESC-FRM-06, signed, and the content recorded, so that what each person was told can be shown.
    The annual refreshers the signed policies require are delivered as recorded toolbox talks or
    briefings: counterfeit materiel (SESC-POL-17 ¶55), product safety (SESC-POL-19 ¶26(b)),
    sanctions (SESC-POL-18 ¶24), modern slavery (SESC-POL-10 ¶14(a)), data protection (SESC-POL-14
    ¶37), and the environmental topics at SESC-POL-08 ¶88. A talk is delivered on the event
    whenever a manufacturer's alert or recall is received, a product-related nonconformity is
    raised or a new product type or manufacturer is introduced (SESC-POL-19 ¶26(c)).

16. The toolbox talk topic list is the Employer's own, in the Employer's titles; no talk carrying
    another organisation's reference is delivered (CLAUDE.md §3; Master Register §2h). The topic
    list for SESC-FRM-06 is drafted under Batch C of the Master Register.

### 4.3 Qualifications, cards and registrations

17. External qualifications, cards and scheme registrations are obtained from the awarding body or
    scheme named in SESC-REG-12, and the certificate is verified with the issuing body before it is
    entered on the Training Matrix: a certificate seen is recorded as seen, and a certificate
    verified is recorded as verified (SESC-FRM-03 `competence_demonstrated`). A certificate issued
    in an incorrect name is not offered as evidence until corrected (SESC-POL-05 ¶173(c)).
    Refresher training is provided at the interval specified by the awarding body, and the interval
    is recorded in SESC-REG-12 from the awarding body's documentation, not assumed (SESC-POL-05
    ¶32).

18. The trade registrations the Employer holds — NICEIC, Gas Safe, MCS, PAS 2030 and F-Gas —
    carry their own competence requirements for the people who work under them, which are met in
    full for the work they cover and recorded in SESC-REG-12 by reference to the scheme standard
    (SESC-POL-16 ¶22; SESC-POL-19 ¶95). The scheme's own assessment and re-assessment is the
    independent oversight of that competence (SESC-POL-19 ¶94(c)).

### 4.4 Training when things change

19. Additional training is provided when work methods, equipment or substances change (SESC-POL-05
    ¶32), when a new trade, product type or kind of location is taken on, when an investigation
    under SESC-PRO-04 ¶14 finds that training or supervision contributed, when a document that
    affects site is issued or revised (SESC-PRO-01 Part 5 stage 9), and when a person changes role.
    The need is recorded on the Training Matrix as a requirement with a date, and the training as
    a SESC-FRM-03 entry when done.

### 4.5 Awareness, as a continuing condition (clause 7.3)

20. Awareness is not a session; it is the state of knowing, on any day, what the policy commits
    to, which objectives the person's work serves, what they contribute, what happens when a
    requirement is not met, and what has gone wrong lately and why. It is maintained by the
    induction at ¶13, the talks at ¶15, the display of the policy statement at Unit 19 (SESC-POL-05
    ¶6), the statutory Health and Safety Law poster (SESC-POL-05 ¶24; SESC-REG-09 CO-21), the
    briefing of lessons from every investigation (SESC-PRO-04 ¶22), and the feedback of what the
    workforce raised (SESC-IMS-05 ¶40). The measure of it is what people do — the near misses they
    report, the work they stop, the items they raise — and those are the measures at SESC-IMS-05
    ¶42 and SESC-IMS-06 OB-H4 and OB-H5.

## Part 5 — Evaluating effectiveness, currency and stand-down (clause 7.2)

21. **Was it effective?** Attendance is not competence (SESC-FRM-03 `competence_demonstrated`,
    "Attendance only"). The effectiveness of training is evaluated and recorded as one of: a
    certificate held and verified with the issuing body; observation of the person doing the work
    by the Site Supervisor or the Contracts Manager, recorded; a knowledge check passed; or, for
    trade work, the inspection, test and commissioning results the work produces and the scheme's
    assessment of it (SESC-POL-19 ¶34(d), (e)). Where the evaluation shows the person is not yet
    competent, the action is repeated, changed or supplemented, and the person is supervised or
    stood down from that activity until it is.

22. **Currency.** The Health and Safety Officer reviews the Training Matrix monthly, actions
    expiring certificates before they lapse (SESC-POL-05 ¶29), and produces from that review the
    list of expired and expiring requirements for the Managing Director, which this procedure adds
    so that the standing objective at ¶24 can be reported; and reviews it at least quarterly
    against the counterfeit materiel requirement (SESC-POL-17 ¶60). Monthly review was committed to
    commence with the month ending 30 September 2026.

23. **Stand-down.** No person is allocated work for which they do not hold current competence.
    Where a certificate has expired the individual is stood down from that activity until it is
    renewed (SESC-POL-05 ¶30), and the stand-down is recorded on the Training Matrix with the date.
    Two stand-downs are already signed: a person whose asbestos awareness certificate has lapsed
    does not attend a building constructed or refurbished before 2000 until it is renewed
    (SESC-POL-05 ¶111, ¶173(a)); and each of the twenty-two expired qualifications is renewed, or the
    person stood down from the activity concerned, by 31 October 2026 (SESC-POL-05 ¶173(b)). The
    position against that date is at Part 7(c).

24. **The measure.** The standing objective is 100 per cent current competence across the Training
    Matrix (SESC-POL-05 ¶46), proposed at SESC-IMS-06 OB-H2 as current ÷ required, reported as
    counts and never names, with 31 December 2026 as the proposed date for reaching it. Training
    currency is an input to the management review (SESC-PRO-03 input 6) and to the first-cycle
    audit IA-02 (SESC-PRO-02 Annex A).
